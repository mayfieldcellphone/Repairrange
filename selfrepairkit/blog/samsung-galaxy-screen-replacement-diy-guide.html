Samsung Galaxy Screen Replacement.

Replacing a Galaxy screen at home ranges from "easy afternoon job" to "take your time" — and which one you're in comes down to whether you've got a flat A-series or a curved S-series flagship. Here's how to tell, and how each goes.

The short answer

Flat Galaxy A-series screens are beginner-friendly. Curved S-series and Ultra flagships are harder — the AMOLED is bonded to a curved frame, so most people fit a screen-and-frame service pack rather than replacing the glass alone, and the under-display fingerprint sensor needs an OEM-grade panel to keep working.

A-series vs S-series: very different jobs

Samsung's two main lines aren't equally easy to repair:

  • Galaxy A-series (flat screens) — flat AMOLED or LCD, usually with a side-button fingerprint sensor. The screen comes as an assembly, lifts off the front, and the fingerprint reader is unaffected. This is the friendly one.
  • Galaxy S-series & Ultra (curved flagships) — curved AMOLED bonded to the metal frame, plus an under-display fingerprint sensor. Separating glass from frame cleanly at home is tough, so the practical route is a service pack (screen + frame together).

What's a "service pack" and why it matters

A service pack is the full screen-and-frame assembly that official repair channels use — the panel already bonded into the mid-frame. For curved flagships, fitting a service pack is far more reliable than trying to peel curved glass off the frame, and it keeps the under-display fingerprint sensor working. For flat A-series phones you usually don't need one — a screen assembly is enough.

Under-display fingerprint: S-series flagships read your fingerprint through the screen, so the sensor is tied to the panel. Use an OEM-grade screen or service pack and re-enrol your print afterward — bargain aftermarket AMOLEDs often break the reader.

How a Samsung screen replacement goes

1. Pick the right part

Screen assembly for a flat A-series; screen-and-frame service pack for a curved S-series/Ultra.

2. Power off and heat the seam

Switch off, then warm the edge you'll open to soften the adhesive.

3. Open the phone

Most Galaxy phones open from the back glass; flat A-series screens can be lifted from the front. Go slowly with picks and suction — Samsung adhesive is strong.

4. Disconnect the battery first

Remove the shields, then unplug the battery before the display cable. Always battery first.

5. Fit the screen or service pack

Install the replacement, reconnect, and test touch, display and fingerprint before sealing.

6. Re-adhere and re-enrol

Apply fresh adhesive, seal it up, and re-enrol your fingerprint.

Pro tip: On a flat A-series, this is a confident first or second repair. On a curved Ultra, give yourself an unhurried hour-plus and test everything before sealing — the adhesive only goes on cleanly once.
