How to Fix the "Non-Genuine Part" Warning After Replacing Your Screen or Battery

You replaced your iPhone screen or battery. Now your phone says "Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple display." Here's what that actually means, what still works, what breaks, and how diagnosable parts fix everything.

The short version

The "non-genuine part" warning is informational, not functional. Your phone works normally. The notification disappears from your lock screen after ~15 days. It stays visible in Settings → General → About → Parts and Service History. If you want it gone completely, you need a diagnosable part paired with Apple's System Configuration tool.

What you'll see after a DIY repair

After replacing the screen or battery on an iPhone 12 or newer, iOS triggers two things:

  1. Lock screen notification — a popup saying "Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple [display/battery]." This appears for approximately 4 days on the lock screen, then moves to Settings for another 11 days, then disappears from notifications entirely.
  2. Parts and Service History entry — a permanent record in Settings → General → About → Parts and Service History. This shows each replaced component and its verification status.

How to check: Settings → General → About → Parts and Service History

Go to Settings → General → About and scroll down. If any parts have been replaced, you'll see a "Parts and Service History" section. Each component shows one of these statuses:

Genuine Apple Part

Apple-original part, installed and paired by Apple or authorised service.

Replaced with Genuine Apple Part ✓

Genuine or diagnosable part, successfully paired using Apple's System Configuration tool. Full functionality restored.

Unknown Part

Part could not be verified. Either non-diagnosable aftermarket, or diagnosable but not yet paired. Phone works normally.

Important Display/Battery Message

Part has a compatibility issue or installation error. Some features may be limited. Re-check cable connections.

What is a "diagnosable" part?

A diagnosable part has an embedded microchip that can communicate with Apple's System Configuration pairing software. When a diagnosable part is installed and then paired, iOS registers it as a verified replacement and clears all warnings.

Here's how it works:

  1. Part has a serial chip — the replacement screen or battery contains a small IC that stores a unique serial number, compatible with Apple's verification system.
  2. System Configuration tool runs — after physical installation, Apple's software (available through Self Service Repair or authorised providers) reads the new part's serial and writes it to the iPhone's logic board.
  3. iOS verifies the pairing — on next boot, iOS checks the logic board records and confirms the part is registered. The "Unknown Part" status changes to verified. True Tone data transfers. Battery Health calibration begins.

How to pair a diagnosable part (remove the warning)

If you've installed a diagnosable screen or battery, here's how to pair it and clear the warning:

Option 1: Apple Self Service Repair (DIY)

  1. Go to support.apple.com/self-service-repair
  2. Start a repair session for your iPhone model
  3. After physically installing the part, use the System Configuration tool (runs on a Mac connected to your iPhone via USB-C)
  4. The tool reads the new part's serial, pairs it with the logic board, and clears the warning
  5. Restart iPhone — Parts and Service History now shows verified status

Option 2: Take it to an Apple Authorised Service Provider

Any Apple Authorised Service Provider (AASP) can run System Configuration on your iPhone — even if you did the physical repair yourself. They charge a small pairing fee (typically $20–$50) without needing to re-do the repair. Call ahead and ask if they offer "third-party part pairing."

Option 3: Third-party pairing tools

Some independent repair shops have third-party tools that can transfer True Tone data and serial information between screens. These tools are widely used in the repair industry but are not officially authorised by Apple. They work well for True Tone restoration but may not clear the Parts and Service History entry on newer iOS versions.

iPhone 15 and newer: Apple has tightened software pairing on iPhone 15+. Battery Health now requires Apple's System Configuration to display the percentage — third-party pairing tools cannot restore this on iPhone 15/16/17. The battery works fine; you just can't see the health percentage without Apple's official pairing.

Can I just ignore the warning?

Yes. Most people do. Here's what happens if you ignore it entirely:

  • Lock screen notification disappears after ~15 days
  • The "Unknown Part" entry stays in Settings → About → Parts and Service History permanently — but nobody looks there
  • Your phone works 100% normally for calls, apps, camera, everything
  • True Tone stays disabled (minor colour warmth difference — most people don't notice)
  • Battery Health shows "Unknown" (battery works fine, you just can't see the percentage)
  • No security implications, no performance throttling, no data at risk

The warning exists because Apple wants you to use their authorised service. It's a business decision, not a safety feature. Your aftermarket screen or battery is functionally identical.

Samsung and OPPO — do they have the same issue?

Samsung: Yes, but less aggressive. Samsung shows a warning notification after screen replacement on Galaxy S21 and newer. It disappears after a few days. There's no "Parts and Service History" equivalent. The main functional issue is that budget INCELL screens disable the ultrasonic fingerprint sensor on Galaxy S-series — this is a hardware limitation, not a software warning. OLED and OEM screens preserve fingerprint. See the Samsung fingerprint guide.

OPPO: Minimal restrictions. OPPO phones generally don't show part verification warnings after screen or battery replacement. Some newer ColorOS versions show a brief notification that disappears within a day. No features are disabled.
