Why Is My Phone Charging So Slowly?

Before you assume the battery's dead and start shopping for a new phone, work through these nine fixes in order — cheapest and easiest first. Most slow-charging problems are a clogged port or a tired cable, and both cost almost nothing to solve.

The short answer

Nine times out of ten it's one of three things: a charging port clogged with lint, a worn or low-wattage cable, or a weak wall adapter. Clean the port, swap to a known-good fast cable, and use a proper charger before you spend a cent on parts. Only if all of that fails is it likely the port hardware or the battery — both of which are cheap DIY repairs.

Work through these in order

1

Clean the charging port (start here — it's free)

Pocket lint compacts into a hard plug at the back of the port and stops the cable seating fully. Power off, shine a light in, and gently scrape it out with a wooden or plastic toothpick from the back forward. Never use metal. This alone fixes a huge share of "sudden" slow charging.

2

Try a different cable

Cables fail internally where they bend, long before they look broken. Swap in a known-good fast-charge cable. If speed jumps, the old cable was the problem — replace it with a braided one that won't fray as fast.

3

Check the wall adapter's wattage

An old 5W block charges any phone at a crawl. Fast charging needs an adapter that supports your phone's standard (usually USB Power Delivery). Plug into a known fast charger and compare. Charging from a laptop USB port or some car sockets is always slow.

4

Stop using the phone while it charges

Gaming, streaming or navigating while plugged in draws power as fast as it comes in. Heat also makes the phone deliberately slow its charging to protect the battery. Put it down, and take it out of a thick case if it's getting warm.

5

Restart the phone

A stuck background process can hold the processor awake and eat charge as it arrives. A simple restart clears it. Free, takes 30 seconds, and worth ruling out.

6

Check for heavy background apps

Look in Settings → Battery for any app using an unusual amount of power. Force-close or update the offender. A misbehaving app can quietly cancel out your entire charging rate.

7

Update the software

Charging behaviour is managed in software, and a buggy update can throttle it. Install any pending OS update — fixes for charging regressions are common.

8

Check your battery health

On iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health. On Samsung: Settings → Battery and device care. Health below ~80%, or a phone that charges fine but drains fast, means a worn battery — see battery draining fast and can you replace a phone battery.

9

Suspect the port hardware

If a clean port, good cable and strong adapter still charge slowly — or the cable only works at a certain angle, feels loose, or falls out — the port itself is worn. On most phones it's a separate flex part you can replace with a DIY kit.

Quick test to isolate the cause: change one thing at a time. New cable fixes it → cable. New adapter fixes it → charger. Cleaning the port fixes it → lint. Nothing helps and battery health is low → battery. Nothing helps but battery is healthy → charging port.

Is it the cable, the port, or the battery?

SymptomMost likely cause
Charges slowly with every cable and chargerClogged port, or worn port / battery
Only charges at a certain angle or falls outWorn charging port
Fast with one cable, slow with anotherCable
Charges full but drains within hoursWorn battery
Slow only from one wall plug or USB portAdapter / power source

If the port is hot, smells burnt, or the phone gets very warm while charging, stop. Unplug it and don't use that charger. Overheating at the port can indicate a damaged port or a faulty charger, both of which are safety issues worth sorting before you keep charging.

When it's the port or the battery

If you've reached fix 8 or 9, the good news is both are inexpensive, DIY-friendly repairs — far cheaper than a new phone. A worn charging port is usually a small flex part, and a worn battery is a 20–45 minute swap. A single bad port or a tired battery almost never justifies replacing the whole phone.
