Can You Replace a Phone Battery? Yes — And Here's Everything You Need to Know.

Most people don't realise their phone battery can be replaced. They assume it's sealed permanently. It's not. A $30–$55 battery kit restores your phone to full-day battery life — and with a diagnosable battery, even Apple's settings will recognise it.

The short answer

Every iPhone, Samsung, and OPPO battery can be replaced. The battery is held by adhesive and connected by a single cable — not soldered or permanently fused. DIY replacement takes 20–45 minutes, costs $22–$55, and gives you another 2–3 years of full battery life. With a diagnosable battery, Battery Health in your iPhone settings works perfectly — showing the real percentage, not "Unknown."

Myth-busting: What people get wrong about battery replacement

"My phone battery can't be replaced — it's sealed inside"

Wrong. Every modern smartphone battery is removable. It's held by adhesive strips (iPhone) or adhesive glue (Samsung/OPPO) and connected by a single press-fit connector. No soldering. The phone was designed to be opened — Apple even offers a Self Service Repair program for battery replacement.

"A replacement battery won't be as good as the original"

Wrong. A quality replacement battery uses the same lithium-ion cell chemistry as the original. Capacity is identical (measured in mAh). Battery life after replacement is the same as day one — your phone will last a full day again. The replacement battery then degrades over 2–3 years at the same rate as the original did.

"I'll lose all my data if I replace the battery"

Wrong. Battery replacement has zero effect on your data. Photos, apps, messages, settings — everything stays exactly as it is. You don't even need to back up (though we recommend it as general good practice). Your phone simply powers off, the battery is swapped, and everything is there when it powers back on.

"It's too hard to do myself"

Partially wrong. Battery replacement is one of the easiest phone repairs — rated 4/10 difficulty for iPhone, 5/10 for Samsung. It's easier than screen replacement because you don't need to transfer any components. The main risk (puncturing the old battery) is avoided by using the pull-tab technique correctly. If you can change a laptop hard drive, you can change a phone battery.

How long will a replacement battery last?

This is the most common question, and the answer is simple: a replacement battery lasts exactly as long as the original did — typically 2–3 years of daily use.

Here's why: all lithium-ion batteries — original, aftermarket, or OEM replacement — follow the same chemistry. They degrade over charge cycles, not calendar time. A typical battery retains:

  • 0–300 cycles (Year 1): 100–95% capacity. Phone lasts all day easily.
  • 300–500 cycles (Year 2): 95–85% capacity. Noticeable decline in heavy use.
  • 500–800 cycles (Year 3): 85–75% capacity. Phone struggles to last a full day.
  • 800+ cycles (Year 3+): Below 80%. Apple recommends replacement. Performance throttling may activate.

A fresh replacement battery starts this cycle over from zero. You get another 2–3 years of full performance — for $22–$55 instead of $1,000+ for a new phone.

Pro tip: To maximise your replacement battery's lifespan, keep it between 20–80% charge when possible, avoid overnight charging on hot surfaces, and use Optimised Battery Charging (iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health → Optimised Battery Charging).

What is a diagnosable battery?

This is where it gets interesting — and where SelfRepairKit batteries stand out from generic eBay/AliExpress cells.

A diagnosable battery has an embedded microchip that communicates with Apple's System Configuration software. When the battery is installed and then paired using Apple's tools, iOS recognises it as a verified replacement. Here's what that means in practice:

How to pair a diagnosable battery with your iPhone

After physically installing a diagnosable battery, you need to run Apple's System Configuration tool to pair it:

  1. Method 1: Apple Self Service Repair — go to support.apple.com/self-service-repair, start a session for your model, connect your iPhone to a Mac via USB-C, and run System Configuration. The tool reads the battery's chip and registers it with the logic board. Takes 5 minutes.
  2. Method 2: Any Apple Authorised Service Provider — bring your phone in (even after a DIY install) and ask them to pair the battery. Typical fee: $20–$50. They run the same tool Apple stores use.
  3. Method 3: Third-party programmer — independent repair shops often have battery programmers that can write serial data to the battery's chip. Works on most models but may not clear the Parts and Service History on iPhone 15+.

The non-genuine battery warning — explained

After installing any non-Apple battery (even a diagnosable one before pairing), your iPhone shows:

"Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple battery"

What happens next:

  • Days 1–4: Notification appears on your lock screen
  • Days 4–15: Moves to Settings → General → About
  • After 15 days: Disappears from notifications entirely
  • Settings → About → Parts and Service History: Shows "Unknown Part" permanently (unless you pair a diagnosable battery, which clears it)

What it does NOT affect: charging speed, battery life, phone performance, app functionality, security, cellular signal, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or anything else. The warning is Apple's way of noting that a non-Apple part was installed. It's informational, not functional.

iPhone 16 and 17: Apple made battery replacement easier

In response to EU regulations requiring user-replaceable batteries by 2025, Apple introduced electrically-induced adhesive debonding starting with the iPhone 16. Instead of pull-tab adhesive strips, the battery is held by a special adhesive that releases when a small electrical current is applied.

What this means for DIY:

  • iPhone 16/17 battery removal is significantly easier — no more wrestling with pull tabs that snap
  • Apply a low-voltage current (9V battery works) to the adhesive contact points and the battery pops free
  • Reduces the risk of puncturing the battery during removal to near-zero
  • The rest of the process (opening the phone, disconnecting, installing new battery) remains the same

For iPhone 7–15, the pull-tab adhesive method still applies. Our iPhone repair guide covers both methods.

Samsung and OPPO batteries

Samsung Galaxy

Samsung batteries are accessed through the back panel (not the front like iPhone). The process takes 30–45 minutes. Samsung uses stronger adhesive than Apple — you'll need isopropyl alcohol to dissolve it. Samsung doesn't currently have a "diagnosable battery" system like Apple, so there's no "Unknown Part" warning to worry about. Samsung Battery Status in settings shows Good/Reduced/Replace regardless of whether the battery is original or aftermarket.

Full guide: How to safely replace Samsung phone battery

OPPO

OPPO batteries are similar to Samsung — back-panel entry, adhesive removal, 30-minute process. OPPO has minimal software restrictions on battery replacement. No persistent warnings, no feature limitations. Battery health in ColorOS settings works normally with aftermarket batteries.

What to look for in a replacement battery

  • Correct mAh capacity — must match or exceed the original. Never buy a battery with lower mAh than your phone's spec. Our kits always match the original capacity.
  • Diagnosable chip (iPhone) — if you want Battery Health to show correctly. SelfRepairKit batteries include this.
  • TI or ATL cells — Texas Instruments and Amperex Technology are the tier-1 cell manufacturers. Avoid no-name cells with suspiciously low prices.
  • Adhesive strips included — you need new adhesive to re-secure the battery. Quality kits include pre-cut strips.
  • Tools included — pentalobe screwdriver (iPhone), Phillips (Samsung), suction cup, spudger. SelfRepairKit includes all tools.
  • Zero-cycle — the battery should have 0 charge cycles (brand new). Some cheap batteries are pulled from used phones and relabelled. Check the product listing.

Avoid $5–$10 batteries. A genuine-quality lithium-ion cell costs $15–$25 wholesale. If a "replacement battery" is selling for $8 on eBay, it's either a recycled cell with reduced capacity, uses cheap chemistry that degrades in 6 months, or is mislabelled with inflated mAh specs. You get what you pay for with batteries — and a bad battery can swell, overheat, or damage your phone.
