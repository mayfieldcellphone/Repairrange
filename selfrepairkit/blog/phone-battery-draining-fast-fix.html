Phone Battery Draining Fast? How to Fix It.

Before you replace the battery, try these 8 software fixes. Most battery drain is caused by settings, not hardware. But if your phone is 2+ years old and nothing helps — we'll show you exactly when it's time for a new battery.

Quick diagnosis

Check battery health first. If it's above 85% → the problem is probably software (try the 8 fixes below). If it's 80–85% → borderline, try software fixes first. If it's below 80% → your battery is degraded and needs replacing. Full battery health guide →

How to check your battery health

📱

iPhone

Settings → Battery → Battery Health → Maximum Capacity

🌌

Samsung

Settings → Battery → Battery Status → shows Good/Reduced/Replace

💚

8 software fixes to try first

These are free, take 5 minutes, and fix 70% of battery drain complaints. Start from the top — the first three make the biggest difference.

1

Turn off background app refresh

iPhone: Settings → General → Background App Refresh → turn off for apps you don't need live updates from. Keep it on for messaging apps only. Samsung: Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → Put unused apps to sleep. Impact: This alone can save 15–25% battery per day.

2

Reduce location services

iPhone: Settings → Privacy → Location Services → set most apps to "While Using" instead of "Always." Samsung: Settings → Location → App Permissions → same approach. GPS is one of the most power-hungry features. Apps like Facebook, Instagram, and weather apps constantly ping your location in the background.

3

Lower screen brightness & timeout

Your screen uses 40–60% of total battery. Turn off auto-brightness and set it to 40–50%. Set screen timeout to 30 seconds. Enable dark mode on OLED phones (iPhone 12+, all Samsung S-series) — dark pixels are literally turned off on OLED, saving significant power.

4

Update your operating system

Apple and Samsung regularly release battery optimization patches. Running an outdated OS can drain 10–20% more. iPhone: Settings → General → Software Update. Samsung: Settings → Software Update. Exception: don't update to a brand-new major release on day one — wait a week for bug fixes.

5

Check battery usage by app

iPhone: Settings → Battery → scroll down to see which apps used the most battery in the last 24 hours. Samsung: Settings → Battery → Battery Usage. Look for apps using more than 10% that you haven't actively used — these are draining in the background. Force-stop or uninstall them.

6

Turn off unnecessary connectivity

Disable features you're not actively using: Bluetooth (when not connected to anything), Wi-Fi (when out of range), AirDrop/Nearby Share (when not transferring), NFC (when not using tap-to-pay). Each one constantly scans for connections and drains power.

7

Disable push email

Push email checks your server constantly. Switch to "Fetch" every 15 or 30 minutes, or "Manual." iPhone: Settings → Mail → Accounts → Fetch New Data. Samsung: Open email app → Settings → Sync schedule. This alone can save 5–10% on heavy email accounts.

8

Factory reset as last resort

If nothing else works and battery health is above 85%, a factory reset eliminates rogue processes and corrupted system files. Back up everything first. iPhone: Settings → General → Transfer or Reset → Erase All Content. Samsung: Settings → General Management → Reset → Factory Data Reset. If battery drain continues after a clean reset, the battery itself needs replacing.

When software fixes won't help

If you've tried all 8 fixes and your battery still drains fast, the problem is hardware. Here are the clear signs your battery needs replacing:

  • Battery health below 80% — the cells are chemically degraded. No software fix can restore lost capacity.
  • Phone dies at 20–30% — the battery gauge is inaccurate because the cells can't hold charge at lower levels.
  • Unexpected shutdowns — especially in cold weather. The battery can't deliver enough current under load.
  • Phone gets hot during normal use — a failing battery generates excess heat.
  • Back panel lifting — the battery is swelling. Stop using immediately and replace.
  • Phone is 2–3+ years old — lithium batteries naturally degrade after 500–800 charge cycles (roughly 2 years of daily charging).
