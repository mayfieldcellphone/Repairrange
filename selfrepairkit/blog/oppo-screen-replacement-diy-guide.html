OPPO Screen Replacement: A DIY Guide.

OPPO screens crack like any other — and replacing one at home is well within reach. The one thing to check before you start is whether your model is LCD or AMOLED, because that decides how careful you need to be with the fingerprint sensor.

The short answer

Yes — OPPO A-series screens come as a full assembly, so swapping one is a realistic home repair. Flat LCD models (often with a side or rear fingerprint sensor) are the easiest. AMOLED models with an under-display fingerprint reader need an OEM-grade screen and a fingerprint re-enrol afterward — but they're still doable.

First, know your panel: LCD or AMOLED

OPPO's A-series spans both screen technologies, and it changes the job:

  • LCD models — common on budget A-series phones, usually paired with a side or rear fingerprint sensor. The screen swap is straightforward and the fingerprint reader is unaffected.
  • AMOLED models — higher-end A-series and Reno phones, often with an under-display fingerprint sensor bonded to the panel. Here the screen you choose matters: an OEM-grade assembly keeps the fingerprint reader and colours correct, while cheap aftermarket AMOLEDs frequently break the sensor.

If you're not sure which you have, check your model's specs — or note where the fingerprint reader is. Reader on the side button or back = LCD-style swap; reader in the screen = treat it as AMOLED.

What you'll need

  • A replacement OPPO screen assembly for your exact model
  • A heat source (heat pad or hairdryer) to release the adhesive
  • Opening picks and a suction cup
  • A precision screwdriver set and a spudger
  • Fresh display adhesive to re-seal

How an OPPO screen replacement goes

1. Back up and power off

Back up your data and switch the phone off fully before opening it.

2. Soften the adhesive

Warm the edges of the screen evenly until the glue loosens. Warm, not hot.

3. Open the phone

Get a suction cup on the glass and slip a pick into the seam, working slowly around the edge. Mind the display ribbon along one side.

4. Disconnect the battery first

Remove any shields, then unplug the battery connector before the display cable. Always battery first.

5. Fit the new screen

Connect the replacement assembly and seat the connector. Reconnect the battery.

6. Test, then seal

Power on and check touch, display and (on AMOLED models) the fingerprint reader before you glue anything. Then apply fresh adhesive and re-enrol your fingerprint if needed.

Pro tip: Test the new screen fully — touch, display, and fingerprint — before you re-adhere it. Two minutes of testing saves peeling a freshly glued screen back off.

Battery safety: Disconnect the battery before working, keep tools away from it, and never bend or puncture it. If the battery is swollen, stop and don't continue the DIY repair.
