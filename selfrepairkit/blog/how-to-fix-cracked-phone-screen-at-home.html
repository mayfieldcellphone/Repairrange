How to Fix a Cracked Phone Screen at Home

The complete step-by-step DIY screen replacement guide for iPhone, Samsung Galaxy, and OPPO. What tools you need, which screen quality to buy, what can go wrong, and how to avoid it.

Quick answer

Yes, you can fix a cracked phone screen at home. An absolute beginner can complete an iPhone screen replacement in 30–45 minutes with the right kit. Samsung takes 45–60 minutes. OPPO takes 30–45 minutes. You'll save 50–70% vs a repair shop. The main risk is damaging biometric sensors (Face ID or fingerprint) — this guide shows you how to avoid that.

What tools do I need to replace a phone screen?

You don't need to buy tools separately. A proper DIY phone repair kit includes everything. But here's what's actually inside the box and why each tool matters:

  • Pentalobe screwdriver (iPhone) or tri-wing screwdriver (Samsung) — these proprietary screws are why you can't use a regular toolkit. Our kits include magnetic precision screwdrivers that prevent losing the microscopic internal screws.
  • Suction cup — industrial-grade vertical pull for safe, damage-free screen separation. Essential for the first step.
  • Plastic spudgers and pry tools — ESD-safe picks to protect fragile flex cables and your phone's logic board from static electricity. Never use metal tools inside a phone.
  • ESD-safe tweezers — for handling tiny screws and connectors without static discharge.
  • Adhesive strips — pre-cut waterproof adhesive to re-seal your phone after repair.
  • Heat source — a hair dryer on low works. Some kits include a heat pad. Heat softens the adhesive holding the screen.

Step-by-step: How to replace a phone screen

This process applies to most iPhones (7–17), Samsung Galaxy S-series (S21–S26), and OPPO models. The exact screw locations differ per model, but the workflow is the same.

1

Power off and remove SIM tray

Always power off completely before opening. Remove the SIM tray — it's easy to forget and it blocks screen removal on some models. Back up your phone to iCloud, Google, or your computer first.

2

Remove bottom screws

iPhone: Two pentalobe screws beside the Lightning/USB-C port. Samsung: Multiple screws around the back panel edge (requires back removal first). OPPO: Similar to Samsung — back panel screws first. Keep screws organised on a magnetic mat or tape them to paper with labels.

3

Heat the edges and apply suction cup

Apply heat around the screen edges for 60–90 seconds using a hair dryer on low. This softens the adhesive. Then attach the suction cup near the bottom edge and pull up gently while sliding a thin pry tool into the gap. Work slowly around all four edges.

⚠️ Warning: Do NOT insert pry tools more than 2mm deep on the top edge of iPhones — the Face ID flex cable sits just below the surface. Cutting this cable permanently disables Face ID.

4

Disconnect battery

Before touching any screen cables, disconnect the battery connector first. This prevents short circuits. Use a plastic spudger — never a metal tool — to gently pry the battery connector upward.

5

Disconnect screen cables and transfer components

Remove the metal bracket covering the display connectors (usually 3–4 tiny Phillips screws). Gently disconnect the display cables by lifting straight up. iPhone: Transfer the earpiece speaker, front camera, and Face ID module from old screen to new — these are NOT included in replacement screens. Samsung: The fingerprint sensor is part of the screen assembly — no transfer needed, but your screen quality tier must support it.

6

Install new screen and apply adhesive

Connect the new screen's flex cables (gentle push until they click). Reconnect battery. Apply the pre-cut adhesive strips around the frame. Carefully lower the new screen into position, pressing around all edges to seat it. Replace bottom screws.

7

Power on and test everything

Before fully sealing, power on and test: touch response across entire screen, Face ID / fingerprint, proximity sensor (make a call and check if screen turns off near your ear), front camera, True Tone (iPhone Settings → Display), and display brightness/colours.

Which screen quality should I buy?

This is the most important decision. The wrong tier wastes money or loses features. Here's the honest breakdown:

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Forcing the screen open too fast. The adhesive needs 60–90 seconds of heat to soften. Rushing tears cables.
  2. Forgetting to disconnect the battery first. Touching live connectors can short-circuit the logic board. Always battery first.
  3. Using metal tools near flex cables. Metal conducts static and scratches PCBs. Only use ESD-safe plastic spudgers.
  4. Buying the wrong screen quality for Samsung. INCELL screens don't support the ultrasonic fingerprint sensor on Galaxy S-series. You'll lose fingerprint unlock permanently. Choose OLED or OEM. Read the Samsung fingerprint guide.
  5. Skipping the test before sealing. Always power on and test touch, Face ID, camera, and sensors BEFORE applying the final adhesive seal.

When should I NOT DIY?

Be honest about these situations:

  • Water damage — if the phone has liquid indicators triggered, internal corrosion needs professional cleaning. Water damage guide.
  • Foldable phones (Samsung Z Fold/Flip) — the folding mechanism is extremely complex. Leave these to professionals.
  • Logic board damage — if the phone doesn't turn on at all, a screen replacement won't fix it.
  • No patience or workspace — if you don't have a clean, well-lit surface and 45 minutes of uninterrupted time, wait until you do.
