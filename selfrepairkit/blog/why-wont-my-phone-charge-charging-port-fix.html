Why Won't My Phone Charge?

Nine times out of ten a phone that "won't charge" isn't broken — it's the cable, the charger, or a plug of lint jammed in the port. Here's how to work through the free fixes first, tell a worn charging port from a tired battery, and replace the port yourself if it comes to that.

The short answer

Before you replace anything, do three things: try a different cable and charger, then clean the port (compacted lint is the single most common cause), then check whether it charges wirelessly. Only if the phone still won't charge with a known-good cable in a clean port are you looking at a worn charging port or a failing battery — and those tell themselves apart by how the phone behaves once it is charging.

First, rule out the easy stuff

The charger and cable fail far more often than the phone. Spend two minutes here before you reach for a screwdriver:

  • Swap the cable. Cables fail internally where they flex near the connector, with no visible damage. Try a different known-good cable.
  • Swap the charger and the outlet. Try a different wall plug, a different power point, or a car charger. A dead brick looks exactly like a dead phone.
  • Try wireless charging (if your phone supports it). If it charges on a pad but not by cable, the problem is the cable or the port — never the battery.
  • Watch for a brief charge icon. If you get a flicker of the charging symbol when you jiggle the cable, the port contacts are the suspect.

The free fix almost everyone skips: clean the port

Over months, pocket lint packs into a hard felt-like plug at the very bottom of the charging port. The cable then can't seat all the way onto the contacts, so it charges intermittently, only at an angle, or not at all. This is the number one cause of a sudden "won't charge" — and it costs nothing to fix.

How to do it safely: Power the phone off. Shine a light into the port — if you see grey fluff packed at the end, gently hook it out with a wooden or plastic toothpick, or loosen it with a dry anti-static brush. Never use a metal pin (it can short the contacts), and never anything wet. You'll often pull out a surprising amount of compacted lint.

Port, battery, or cable? How to tell them apart

Once you've cleaned the port and ruled out the cable, the phone's behaviour points to the culprit:

  • Only charges when you wiggle, push, or hold the cable — or only one way up on USB-C → the charging port. A healthy USB-C port charges identically in both orientations.
  • Charges fine but the battery drops fast, dies suddenly around 20–40%, or the back is swelling → the battery, not the port.
  • One cable works and another doesn't → it was simply the cable.
  • Charges on a wireless pad but not by cable → port or cable, never the battery.
  • Won't transfer files to a computer either → the port (data and power share the same connector).

Signs the charging port itself needs replacing

If you've cleaned it and swapped cables and it still misbehaves, replace the port when you see:

  • You have to wiggle or prop the cable to keep it charging
  • It only charges in one USB-C orientation
  • Visibly bent, pushed-in, or corroded pins inside the port
  • Charging cuts in and out even with a brand-new cable
  • No data connection to a PC at all

Replacing a charging port yourself

This is a more involved repair than a battery or screen, but on many phones it needs no soldering — the port is a modular part. Here's the shape of the job:

1. Power off completely

Shut the phone down fully before opening it — never work on a live device.

2. Open the housing with heat

Soften the adhesive around the edges with a heat pad or hairdryer, then work in opening picks with a suction cup. Most modern phones open from the back glass or the screen — go slowly and evenly.

3. Disconnect the battery first

Always unplug the battery connector before touching anything else. This is the single most important safety step inside any phone.

4. Locate and free the port

On many Samsung Galaxy models the USB-C port lives on a separate charging sub-board joined by a ribbon cable — unclip it and lift it straight out. On most iPhones and some others it's a flex cable running into the phone. Either way, note the routing before you remove it.

5. Fit the new port and reconnect

Seat the replacement, clip its ribbon back into place, then reconnect the battery last.

6. Test before you seal

Power on and confirm it charges and transfers data in both cable orientations before you re-adhere the housing. Testing first saves opening it twice.

Battery safety: Lithium batteries can catch fire if bent, punctured, or crushed. Disconnect the battery before working, keep sharp tools away from it, and if your battery is already swollen, stop — don't attempt a DIY repair on a swollen battery. Work on a clean, static-safe surface.

Samsung charging-port repairs are the beginner-friendly ones

Because so many Samsung Galaxy phones put the USB-C port on a modular charging sub-board rather than soldering it to the mainboard, swapping it is one of the most approachable internal repairs there is — unclip, replace, reconnect. No microsoldering, no special station.
