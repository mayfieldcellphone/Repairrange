Can I Change My iPhone Battery Myself?

Yes. And it's easier than you think. This guide covers every iPhone from 7 to 17 — when to replace, what it costs, the exact steps, and the one mistake that can set your phone on fire.

Quick answer

iPhone battery replacement is rated 4/10 difficulty — easier than screen replacement. Takes 20–30 minutes. Costs $22–$45 for a DIY kit vs $109–$119 at Apple. No data loss. The main risk is puncturing the old battery during removal — follow the pull-tab method and you'll be fine.

How do I know my iPhone battery needs replacing?

Go to Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging. Look at Maximum Capacity:

  • 95–100% — Battery is fine. No action needed.
  • 80–95% — Noticeable decline. Phone lasts 70–80% of original. Optional replacement.
  • Below 80% — Apple recommends replacement. iOS enables performance throttling. Replace now.
  • "Service" message — Battery is significantly degraded. Replace immediately.

Other signs that don't show in settings: phone dies at 20–30% charge, shuts down unexpectedly in cold weather, feels hot during normal use, or the back glass is slightly lifting outward (swollen battery — stop using immediately and replace).

Step-by-step: Replace your iPhone battery

This process works for iPhone 7 through iPhone 17. The screw positions differ slightly per model, but the workflow is identical.

1

Power off and remove bottom screws

Power off completely. Remove the two pentalobe screws beside the charging port. These are tiny — use the magnetic pentalobe screwdriver from your kit and place screws on the magnetic mat.

2

Open the phone

Apply the suction cup near the bottom edge. Pull up gently while sliding a thin pry tool into the gap. iPhone 7–SE3: screen opens from left like a book. iPhone 12–17: screen opens from bottom upward. Don't open past 90° — the display cables are still connected at the top.

3

Disconnect battery

Remove the metal bracket over the battery connector (2–3 Phillips screws). Use a plastic spudger to pop the battery connector straight up. This de-powers the phone — essential before any other work.

4

Remove old battery using pull tabs

iPhones use adhesive pull tabs to hold the battery. Find the tab edges (usually at the bottom of the battery) and pull them slowly at a low angle — almost parallel to the battery surface. Pull slowly and steadily. If a tab snaps, apply a small amount of isopropyl alcohol (90%+) along the edge and wait 60 seconds for it to dissolve the adhesive.

⚠️ Critical: NEVER pry under the battery with metal tools. Lithium batteries can puncture, swell, smoke, or catch fire if the internal cell is pierced. Only use plastic spudgers. If the battery is visibly swollen, wear safety glasses and work in a well-ventilated area.

5

Install new battery

Apply the new adhesive strips to the back of the replacement battery (they come pre-cut in your kit). Lower the battery into position — align it precisely in the battery well. Press firmly. Connect the battery connector (press down until it clicks). Replace the metal bracket and screws.

6

Close and test

Lower the screen back into position. Replace the two bottom pentalobe screws. Power on. Your phone will show "Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple battery" — this is normal for all non-Apple-installed batteries. Battery Health will show "Unknown" for 1–2 weeks while iOS recalibrates.

What about Battery Health calibration?

After a DIY battery replacement, iOS will display "Unknown" battery health for approximately 1–2 weeks. This is normal — iOS needs several full charge cycles to calculate the new battery's maximum capacity. During this period, your phone works perfectly — it just can't display the health percentage yet.

On iPhone 15 and newer, Apple has introduced software-level battery pairing. A non-Apple-installed battery will permanently show "Unknown" for Battery Health, though the battery itself works normally. Apple's own service can re-pair the battery using their proprietary software.

Is it worth doing myself or should I go to Apple?

DIY makes sense if: your phone is out of warranty, you want to save $60–$90, and you're comfortable following a 20-minute guide. Battery replacement is genuinely one of the easiest phone repairs.

Apple makes sense if: you have AppleCare+ (free replacement below 80%), you want the Battery Health reading to calibrate properly (iPhone 15+), or you're not confident handling small screws and adhesive.

A local shop makes sense if: you want it done in 30 minutes, don't have the time/patience for DIY, but don't want to pay Apple's premium. Find a good shop: how to find a trustworthy repair shop.
