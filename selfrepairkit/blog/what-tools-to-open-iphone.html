What Tools Do I Need to Open an iPhone?

You can't use a regular screwdriver. Apple uses proprietary screws specifically designed to keep you out. Here's every tool you actually need, what each one does, and why a $3 screwdriver unlocks a $200+ saving.

Quick answer

You need 7 tools to open and repair any iPhone: P2 pentalobe screwdriver, Y000 tri-point screwdriver, Phillips #000 screwdriver, suction cup, plastic spudgers, ESD-safe tweezers, and adhesive strips. A complete phone repair tool kit costs $15–40 standalone. Every SelfRepairKit includes all 7 tools free.

The 7 essential iPhone repair tools

1. P2 Pentalobe Screwdriver

ESSENTIAL — Used on every iPhone repair

The pentalobe has a 5-pointed star tip — Apple designed it specifically to stop people opening iPhones. The P2 size fits every iPhone from iPhone 4 to iPhone 17. It removes the two bottom case screws beside the charging port. This is always the first and last tool you use.

Can I use a regular screwdriver? No. Phillips, flathead, and Torx won't fit. The pentalobe shape is unique to Apple products. Trying to force a wrong screwdriver will strip the screw heads permanently.

2. Y000 Tri-Point Screwdriver

INTERNAL — For bracket screws (iPhone 7+)

The tri-point has a 3-pronged Y-shaped tip. Apple introduced it with the iPhone 7 for the internal bracket screws that cover display and battery connectors. Without this, you can open the phone but can't disconnect anything safely.

3. Phillips #000 Screwdriver

INTERNAL — For component screws

Standard Phillips cross-head, but in the smallest size (#000). Used for various internal bracket screws. Some models use more Phillips screws than others. Older iPhones (7, 8) use them heavily; newer models use more tri-point.

4. Suction Cup

ESSENTIAL — Screen separation

Industrial-grade suction cup that attaches to the screen surface. After removing the bottom screws and heating the edges, you pull the suction cup to create a gap between the screen and frame. Then slide a pry tool in. Without a proper suction cup, you'll struggle to get the initial separation — fingernails don't work.

5. Plastic Spudgers & Pry Picks

ESSENTIAL — Cable disconnection & prying

Flat plastic tools for two jobs: (1) sliding around the screen edge to cut through adhesive, and (2) popping flex cable connectors off the logic board. Must be plastic, never metal. Metal tools conduct static electricity (kills chips), scratch circuit boards, and can puncture batteries.

ESD-safe spudgers are treated to prevent static discharge. Regular plastic tools from the hardware store don't have this protection.

6. ESD-Safe Tweezers

PRECISION — For tiny screws & connectors

Fine-tip tweezers with anti-static coating. iPhone screws are 1–2mm — too small for fingers. Tweezers let you place and retrieve screws precisely. The ESD-safe coating prevents static discharge that could damage the logic board. Our kits include magnetic precision screwdrivers that also help with screw handling.

7. Adhesive Strips

FINISHING — Re-seal after repair

Pre-cut waterproof adhesive strips that go around the frame edge to re-seal your phone. The original adhesive is destroyed when you open the phone. Without new adhesive, dust and moisture can enter. Our kits include model-specific pre-cut strips — no trimming needed.

Nice-to-have tools (not essential)

  • Magnetic screw mat — keeps screws organised by position. Prevents losing the 12+ microscopic screws inside an iPhone. Highly recommended for first-timers. Available as an add-on.
  • Heat pad or hair dryer — softens screen adhesive. A hair dryer on low works fine. Heat pads offer more consistent, hands-free heating.
  • iOpener or heat bag — microwave-heated bag placed on the screen edge. Alternative to hair dryer.
  • Magnifying glass or head loupe — helpful if you have vision issues. The screws are genuinely tiny.

Tools to NEVER use inside an iPhone:

  • Metal screwdrivers as pry tools — scratches PCB traces, shorts components
  • Kitchen knives or razor blades — cuts cables, punctures battery
  • Regular pliers — too aggressive for delicate connectors
  • Magnetic screwdrivers near the display — can interfere with sensors (minor risk, but worth noting)

Do Samsung and OPPO use the same tools?

Mostly, but not the pentalobe. Samsung and OPPO use standard Phillips screws externally, so you don't need the proprietary pentalobe screwdriver. The rest of the toolkit (suction cup, spudgers, tweezers, adhesive) is identical. Samsung and OPPO also require a heat source more critically than iPhone because you enter through the back glass, which is heavily glued.

A quality phone repair tool kit works across all three brands — the only iPhone-specific tool is the pentalobe screwdriver.
