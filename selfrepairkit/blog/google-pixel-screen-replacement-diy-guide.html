Google Pixel Screen Replacement: A DIY Guide.

Cracked the screen on your Pixel? It's a very doable home repair — but Pixels have one quirk that decides whether it goes smoothly: the fingerprint reader lives under the glass. Here's what that means, why the screen you choose matters more than the technique, and how the job actually goes.

The short answer

Yes, you can replace a Pixel screen yourself. From the Pixel 6 onward the fingerprint reader is built into the display, so the single biggest factor in success isn't your skill — it's using a genuine OEM screen. Fit an OEM assembly and the fingerprint sensor, Smooth Display and colours all keep working (you just re-enrol your fingerprint afterward). Fit a cheap aftermarket panel and the fingerprint reader often dies. That's why our Pixel kits are OEM only.

Can you replace a Pixel screen yourself?

Comfortably, yes — it's a moderate-difficulty repair that takes most people 45 to 90 minutes the first time. The work itself is the same family of steps as any modern phone: heat to soften the adhesive, open carefully, disconnect the battery, swap the panel, re-seal. What sets the Pixel apart is the fingerprint sensor under the glass, which is entirely manageable as long as you use the right screen.

Why the screen you choose matters more on a Pixel

On most phones a cheaper screen just means slightly different colours. On a Pixel it can mean lost features, because three things are tuned to the original OLED panel:

  • The under-display fingerprint sensor (Pixel 6 and newer) — bonded to the display and paired to it.
  • Smooth Display — the 90Hz or 120Hz high refresh rate on Pro models.
  • Adaptive brightness and colour accuracy — calibrated to the panel.

Bargain aftermarket Pixel panels routinely break the fingerprint reader, drop to 60Hz, or show washed-out colours. A genuine OEM assembly keeps all of it. For a Pixel specifically, OEM isn't a luxury — it's the difference between a phone that works properly afterward and one that doesn't.

The under-display fingerprint sensor: On the Pixel 6 and every Pixel since, the reader is part of the screen, not a separate component. With an OEM screen the sensor comes attached and works after you re-enrol your fingerprint in Settings. With a low-cost aftermarket screen it may never pair. (The Pixel 5 and earlier use a rear fingerprint sensor, so they're unaffected by a screen swap.)

What the job needs

A proper Pixel screen kit includes everything below — if you're sourcing parts separately, make sure you have:

  • An OEM screen assembly for your exact Pixel model
  • A heat source (heat pad or hairdryer) to soften the adhesive
  • Opening picks and a suction cup
  • A precision screwdriver set and a spudger
  • Fresh display adhesive to re-seal

How a Pixel screen replacement goes

1. Back up and power off

Back up your phone and shut it down fully. Never open a phone that's still on.

2. Soften the adhesive

Warm the edges of the screen evenly with a heat pad or hairdryer until the adhesive loosens. Warm, not hot — you're softening glue, not cooking the phone.

3. Open from the front

Most Pixels open at the screen. Get a suction cup on the glass, slip a pick into the seam, and work slowly around the edge. The display ribbon runs along one side — go gently there so you don't tear it.

4. Disconnect the battery first

Remove any metal shields, then unplug the battery connector before you disconnect the display cable. Battery first, always.

5. Fit the OEM screen

Connect the new OEM display assembly — the fingerprint sensor comes with it — and seat the connector firmly. Reconnect the battery.

6. Test, then seal and re-enrol

Power on and check touch, display, and the fingerprint reader before you glue anything. Then apply fresh adhesive, seal the screen, re-enrol your fingerprint, and confirm Smooth Display is running.

Pro tip: Always do a full test — touch across the whole screen, brightness, and a fingerprint enrolment — before you re-adhere the panel. It's a two-minute check that saves you peeling a freshly glued screen back off.

Which Pixels are DIY-friendly?

The whole mainstream line is approachable: Pixel 3 through Pixel 10, including the a-series and the Pro and Pro XL models. The two to leave to a professional are the foldables — the Pixel 9 Pro Fold and 10 Pro Fold — whose flexible inner displays and hinges make them an expert-level repair, not a first one.
