Phone Got Wet? What to Do.

The first ten minutes decide whether your phone survives — and the two most common reactions (charging it to "check," and burying it in rice) are exactly the two things that make it worse. Here's what actually helps.

The short answer

Get it out, power it off, and do not charge it. Wipe it down, pull the SIM tray, and let it air-dry for 24–48 hours with a fan or silica gel — not rice. Only power it on once it's fully dry. If a single part failed afterward (battery, screen, or charging port), that's a replaceable DIY fix.

Do this immediately

1. Get it out and power off

Pull it from the water and switch it off straight away. Don't keep using it or pressing buttons — that pushes water deeper and risks a short.

2. Do NOT charge it

Plugging a wet phone into power is the fastest way to kill the board. No charging until it's completely dry.

3. Dry the outside, open it up

Wipe it down, remove the SIM/card tray to open an airflow path, and gently shake water out of the ports (port facing down).

4. Air-dry for 24–48 hours

Stand it in front of a fan, or seal it in a bag with silica-gel packets. Then wait. Patience is the actual fix.

Why rice doesn't work

The rice trick is the most repeated phone myth there is, and it's largely useless. Rice absorbs ambient moisture far too slowly to beat the corrosion that starts inside the phone within minutes, and rice dust and starch can lodge in the charging port and speaker mesh — adding a new problem. Phones that "survived the rice" usually survived because of the time, not the rice. Airflow and a real desiccant (silica gel) do the same job better, and doing nothing but waiting beats rice too.

Don't: charge it, power it on to "test," press buttons, shake it hard, or blast it with a hairdryer on heat — heat can warp components and adhesive. And skip the rice.

The hidden danger: corrosion

Here's why a phone can seem fine after a dunking and then die days later. As the water dries, it leaves mineral deposits that slowly corrode the circuit board and connectors over the following days. That's also why salt water and sugary drinks are far worse than clean water — they're more conductive and more corrosive. So even if it powers on, keep an eye out for faults developing over the next week.

What you can fix yourself afterward

Once it's dry and you've powered it on, water damage tends to show up as one of a few specific, replaceable faults:

  • Battery — swollen, draining fast, or won't hold charge. A battery kit fixes it.
  • Screen — lines, dead patches, discoloured areas, or no display. A screen kit fixes it.
  • Charging port — corroded contacts that won't charge. A charging-port kit fixes it.

What you generally can't fix at home is widespread board-level corrosion, which needs professional micro-soldering. So the smart play is: dry it, see what actually broke, and if it's one of the parts above, swap it.
