Your phone repair rights in Australia: what you can actually claim.

By RepairRange · how we price · Updated 15 August 2026

Third-party repairs don't void your warranty. Retailers can't refuse a claim because an independent shop fixed your phone. And consumer guarantees outlast manufacturer warranties. Here's the plain-English version of the Australian Consumer Law for phone owners.

Consumer guarantees beat manufacturer warranties

Your phone comes with two layers of protection. The manufacturer warranty (Apple's one-year coverage, Samsung's, etc.) is what the brand promises on top. The consumer guarantees are automatic rights under the Australian Consumer Law — they can't be taken away by any warranty wording, and they apply for as long as the product should reasonably last, not just 12 months.

✅ The short version: if your phone isn't of acceptable quality, doesn't match its description, or doesn't last a reasonable time, the seller must fix it — repair, replace or refund. That obligation survives the one-year "warranty" sticker.

The big one: third-party repairs do NOT void your warranty

This is the most repeated myth in Australian phone repair. Having an independent shop replace your screen or battery does not void your warranty or consumer guarantees. The ACCC has made its position clear, and telling customers otherwise can itself be a breach of the law. If a repairer or retailer refuses a claim because a third party touched the device, push back — and point them to the ACCC's consumer guarantees page.

⚠️ The genuine caveat: if the third-party repair itself causes the fault (a bad install that damages the logic board), that damage isn't covered. Your rights still apply to the original, pre-existing fault.

What counts as "a reasonable time"?

There's no fixed number of years — the law looks at the price and type of product. A premium flagship that fails after 14 months is far more likely to attract a remedy than a budget handset at the same age. Consumer regulators have consistently treated mid-to-premium smartphones as expected to last several years. Keep your receipt, purchase confirmation or bank statement — proof of purchase is all you need, not the box or the original warranty card.

What you can claim, and from whom

  • Major failure (serious problem, or can't be fixed within a reasonable time): you choose a replacement or refund.
  • Minor failure: the retailer can choose to repair it within a reasonable time, free of charge.
  • Consequential costs: you can also recover reasonable costs you incurred because of the failure — including what you paid a repairer to fix a fault the seller was responsible for.
  • Who to go to: the retailer who sold you the phone has direct responsibility — they can't fob you off to Apple or Samsung. The manufacturer also has obligations, but the retailer is your first port of call.

Repair, replace or refund: how to make the claim

  1. Contact the retailer with proof of purchase, describe the fault, and ask them to repair it under the consumer guarantees.
  2. If they refuse or stall, escalate in writing (email is fine) and reference the Australian Consumer Law consumer guarantees.
  3. If they still refuse, contact your state/territory consumer affairs body, or lodge a complaint with the ACCC / Australian Financial Complaints Authority equivalents in your state. Useful starting points: Consumer Affairs Victoria and the ACCC.

What this means for where you repair

Because your rights don't hinge on using an authorised repairer, the "repair it at Apple or lose your warranty" pressure is legally hollow. The practical decision is cost and quality: see Apple official vs third-party repair — the full cost comparison for the numbers, and screen quality tiers so you know what you're being quoted. And if a repair quote approaches the value of the phone, check repair vs replace before you spend.

FAQ

Repair rights: common questions

Does repairing my phone at a third-party shop void my warranty in Australia?

No. Under the Australian Consumer Law, an independent repair does not void your warranty or consumer guarantees, and a supplier can't refuse a claim just because a third party repaired the device.

How long does a phone have to last before I can claim?

There's no fixed number — it depends on price and type. Regulators treat mid-range to premium phones as reasonable to last several years, with longer expectations for expensive flagships.

What can I claim — repair, replacement or refund?

For a major failure you choose a refund or replacement. For a minor failure the seller repairs it within a reasonable time. You can also recover reasonable costs caused by the failure.

Who is responsible — the retailer or the manufacturer?

Both have obligations, but the retailer who sold you the phone has direct responsibility for the consumer guarantees — they can't pass you to the manufacturer.

What if the retailer refuses to fix my phone?

Escalate in writing and reference the consumer guarantees, then contact your state consumer affairs body or the ACCC. Keep proof of purchase — that's all you legally need.
