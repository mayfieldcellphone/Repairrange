Phone insurance vs self-insuring — the actual maths.

AppleCare+ costs $279 for 2 years. Samsung Care+ costs $264–$360. Telstra Device Protect costs $360–$480. But a screen repair at an independent shop costs $199–$549 — once. Here's the honest maths on when insurance makes sense and when you're literally paying to feel better.

The self-insuring concept

Self-insuring means skipping insurance entirely and paying for repairs out of pocket when they happen. Instead of $15/month going to an insurer, you put that $15 into a savings account. If nothing breaks, you keep the money. If something breaks, you pay for the repair from your fund. Statistically, most people come out ahead self-insuring because most people don't break their phones often enough for insurance to pay off.

The industry data supports this: phone insurance claim rates average 15–25% per year across all customers. That means 75–85% of insured customers pay premiums and never make a claim. Insurance companies are profitable precisely because most customers don't break their phones.

AppleCare+ — the best value manufacturer plan

Cost: $279 for 2 years (or $14.49/month)

Covers: 2 incidents of accidental damage per year. Screen repair excess: $45. Other damage excess: $149. Battery replacement: free if below 80% capacity. Also extends manufacturer warranty to 2 years. Does NOT cover: Theft or loss.

The maths — iPhone 17 Pro Max:

AppleCare+ total cost for one screen claim: $279 (premium) + $45 (excess) = $324.

Screen repair at indie shop: $349–$549. At Apple without AppleCare+: $379.

Verdict: AppleCare+ saves $25–$225 if you crack the screen once. It genuinely pays for itself on Pro Max models. Worth it for: iPhone 15 Pro/Pro Max and newer.

The maths — iPhone 15 (standard):

AppleCare+ total cost: $279 + $45 = $324.

Screen repair at indie shop: $199–$289.

Verdict: AppleCare+ costs MORE than the repair. You're paying $324 to avoid a $199–$289 expense. Not worth it for standard iPhones.

Samsung Care+ — only worth it on foldables

Cost: $10.99–$14.99/month

Covers: Accidental damage. Screen repair excess: $99. Other damage excess: $199. Does NOT cover: Theft or loss (separate Samsung Care+ with Theft & Loss plan available).

The maths — Galaxy S26 Ultra:

Samsung Care+ total cost for one screen claim (24 months): $360 (premiums at $14.99/mo) + $99 (excess) = $459.

Screen repair at indie shop: $399–$619. Samsung authorised: ~$449.

Verdict: Samsung Care+ costs $459 to cover a repair that costs $399–$619. It barely breaks even if the indie repair is expensive ($619), but it's actually more expensive than Samsung's own authorised price ($449). Borderline — not clearly worth it.

The maths — Galaxy Z Fold 6 (this is where it changes):

Samsung Care+ total cost (24 months): $360 + $99 = $459.

Inner screen repair without insurance: $599–$899.

Verdict: Samsung Care+ saves $140–$440 on a single inner screen claim. Genuinely worth it on foldable phones.

Carrier insurance — Telstra, Optus, Vodafone

Telstra Device Protect: $15–$20/month | Excess: $99–$199

Covers: Accidental damage, mechanical breakdown, AND theft/loss (the key differentiator). Total cost for one claim (24 months): $360–$480 premiums + $99–$199 excess = $459–$679. Verdict: Too expensive for accidental damage alone (indie screen repair is cheaper). Only makes sense if theft/loss coverage is important to you.

Optus Device Protection: $12–$17/month | Excess: $99–$199

Similar to Telstra. Covers accidental damage + theft/loss. Total cost: $288–$408 premiums + excess = $387–$607 for one claim. Slightly cheaper than Telstra but same analysis applies.

The problem with carrier insurance: You're paying $15–$20/month regardless of whether you make a claim, and the excess is high enough that the total cost often matches or exceeds the indie repair price. The only unique value is theft/loss coverage — if your phone is stolen, insurance replaces it (minus the excess). If you've never had a phone stolen, you're paying for coverage you'll statistically never use.

Self-insuring: the maths that matter

The self-insurance fund: Put $15/month into a savings account. After 24 months: $360 saved. That's enough to cover any single phone repair — screen, battery, port, or back glass on virtually any model. And if nothing breaks, you keep the $360.

The probability factor: If you break your phone screen once every 3 years (the average for careful users), self-insuring gives you $540 saved over 3 years — more than enough for a flagship screen repair with money left over. Insurance would have cost you $540 in premiums over the same period, plus the excess when you claim.

When self-insuring loses: If you break your phone more than once per year (serial dropper), or if you need theft/loss coverage, or if you own a foldable (repair costs are extreme enough to justify insurance).

The decision framework

Buy AppleCare+ if: You own an iPhone 15 Pro or newer (screen repair exceeds AppleCare+ cost), you want the peace of mind, and you value Apple's guaranteed Face ID preservation and genuine parts.

Buy Samsung Care+ if: You own a foldable (Z Fold, Z Flip). The inner screen repair savings genuinely justify the premiums. Skip it for standard Galaxy S-series — the maths don't work.

Buy carrier insurance if: You work in an environment where theft/loss is a real risk (construction sites, nightlife, travel). Carrier insurance is the only common plan that covers theft and loss. If accidental damage is your only concern, it's overpriced.

Self-insure if: You use a case and screen protector, you rarely break phones, you own a standard (non-foldable) phone, and you're comfortable paying for a repair if it happens. Put $15/month aside. Most people will come out ahead over 3–5 years.

What about home and contents insurance?

Many Australians don't realise their home and contents insurance may already cover accidental phone damage and theft — both at home and when you're out. Check your policy's "portable contents" or "personal effects" section. Some policies cover phones with no additional premium and an excess of $100–$300. If your contents insurance already covers your phone, any separate phone insurance is redundant.

Tip: Call your insurer and ask specifically: "Does my home and contents policy cover accidental damage to my phone away from home?" If yes, you may already be covered without paying for AppleCare+, Samsung Care+, or carrier insurance.
