Water Damage: What a Repair Shop Can and Can't Recover

When a phone, laptop, or tablet takes an unexpected swim, the immediate aftermath is often filled with panic. We have all heard the advice about placing devices in rice, but professional repairers often find that these household remedies do more harm than good. Water damage is rarely just about the water itself; it is about the chemical reaction that occurs when electricity meets moisture and minerals. Understanding the reality of internal corrosion is essential for deciding whether your device can be saved or if it is time to move on.

The Chemistry of Corrosion

The primary enemy of a water-damaged device is not the water, but the minerals and impurities held within it. Tap water, pool water, and especially saltwater contain conductive minerals. When these minerals enter the device while the battery is still providing power, they cause electrolysis. This process accelerates the oxidisation of metallic components on the logic board. Within hours, these minerals form a crusty, conductive layer that bridges connections that should never touch, leading to short circuits.

A professional repair shop uses ultrasonic cleaners and high-grade isopropyl alcohol to remove these deposits. However, the window for this to be effective is narrow. The longer the device remains powered or damp, the deeper the corrosion eats into the microscopic traces on the motherboard. If the oxidisation has progressed far enough, it can cause 'via rot', where the tiny copper pathways inside the layers of the circuit board are severed. Once these traces are broken, the damage is typically permanent.

What Can Be Recovered

Professional technicians generally view water damage repairs in two categories: functional repair and data recovery. If you are looking to get the device back into daily working order, the technician needs to ensure the logic board is stable. If the board is cleaned quickly enough, the device might function exactly as it did before. In many cases, replacing the battery is a mandatory part of this process, as lithium-ion cells are highly susceptible to instability after moisture exposure.

Data recovery is often a more successful endeavour than full device restoration. If the central processor (CPU) and the storage (NAND flash) remain intact, a skilled repairer can often perform 'micro-soldering' to bypass damaged power circuits, allowing them to pull your photos, contacts, and documents off the chip. Even if the screen, cameras, and speakers are permanently broken, the core data can often be extracted. The Limits of Repair

Not everything can be saved, and sometimes the cost-to-benefit ratio makes professional repair impractical. Components like microphones, speakers, and cameras are notoriously difficult to clean because they are encased in mesh or sealed housings. If water gets inside these parts, they usually need to be replaced entirely. If the device was submerged in saltwater or sugary liquids like soft drinks, the corrosive potential is exponentially higher, often leaving behind a sticky, acidic residue that destroys solder joints rapidly.

Another factor is the structural integrity of the device. If the chassis has been warped by heat or impact, or if the screen digitiser has delaminated, the device may never be properly sealed again. Deciding on the Next Step

When you take a water-damaged device to a shop, the technician will usually perform a diagnostic to see if the device draws power and if the logic board is shorting. They will look for signs of 'hot spots' where components have burnt out. If you are unsure whether the repair is worth the investment, ask the technician for a clear assessment of the board's condition rather than just a quote. A board that shows widespread corrosion is a poor candidate for long-term repair, whereas a device that suffered a quick spill and was powered off immediately has a much higher success rate.

If the repair is deemed too risky or costly, you might choose to replace the unit. In that scenario, focus on salvaging what you can, such as the SIM card or the SD card if the device supports them. Regardless of the outcome, the most important lesson is that power is the enemy. If your device gets wet, shut it down immediately, stop trying to turn it back on to 'test it', and get it to a professional who can open the housing and safely remove the moisture before the chemical reaction takes hold.
