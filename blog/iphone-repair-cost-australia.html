iPhone Repair Cost in Australia (2026): What You'll Actually Pay

By RepairRange · how we price · Updated 19 August 2026

Cracked screen, dying battery, or a port that won't charge — here's what Australians actually pay to fix an iPhone in 2026, model by model, and how to tell a fair quote from an inflated one.

iPhone repair cost at a glance

The honest answer to "how much is an iPhone repair?" is: it depends heavily on which iPhone you have, which part is broken, and whether you go through Apple, an authorised provider, or an independent repairer. Below is a plain-English breakdown of what Australians are actually paying in 2026, model by model, so you can tell a fair quote from an inflated one before you hand your phone over.

Back glass pricing varies the most of any iPhone repair — see our dedicated iPhone back glass repair cost guide for the model-by-model breakdown, including why the iPhone 12 is so expensive to fix.

Older, smaller-screen models sit at the bottom of these ranges; the newest Pro and Pro Max models sit at the top, especially for screens, where Apple's own out-of-warranty pricing on the current Pro Max sits at $379 and premium independent options can run to ~$549.

Battery replacement cost by model

Batteries are cheaper and more standardised than screens, which is why this is often the best-value repair on an older phone.

If your iPhone is otherwise in good shape and you're mainly dealing with poor battery life or the phone shutting down unexpectedly, a battery swap is usually the highest-value repair you can do — it's a fraction of the cost of a screen and it's the fix most likely to make an older phone feel new again.

Other common repairs

  • Charging port repair: $60–$150. Often actually a cleaning/debris issue rather than a hardware failure — worth a free diagnostic before paying for a part swap.
  • Camera repair: $100–$420, with rear camera assemblies on Pro/Pro Max models (which have multiple lenses and sensors) at the top of that range.
  • Back glass repair: $120–$300+. Frequently underestimated — since the iPhone X, back glass replacement on many models requires more disassembly than a front screen swap, which is part of why it isn't always cheaper than it looks.
  • Water damage: $120–$350+, and often quoted as an assessment fee first since the outcome (and real cost) depends on what the liquid actually reached.

What actually moves the price

Four things explain almost all of the spread in the tables above: model (Pro/Pro Max parts cost more than standard models), part quality (genuine Apple parts vs. aftermarket — aftermarket is cheaper but can affect True Tone, Face ID, or long-term durability depending on the repairer), damage extent (a clean crack vs. a phone that's also bent, water-affected, or has failed multiple components), and who does the work (Apple/Apple Authorised Service Providers price at the top of the market; independent local repairers are usually 30–60% cheaper for the same fault).

Is it worth repairing or replacing?

As a rough rule of thumb: if the repair cost is under 25–30% of what a comparable used or new-condition replacement would cost, and the phone is otherwise working well, repair is almost always the better financial call. Once you're looking at a $300+ screen on a phone that's already three or four generations old, it's worth comparing that quote against trade-in value or a second-hand upgrade — we cover this trade-off in more detail in our repair vs. replace guide.

AppleCare+ vs. paying out of pocket

If you're not already covered, AppleCare+ is worth doing the maths on rather than assuming either way: the annual/monthly premium plus the reduced excess only pays off if you're actually likely to need a repair (screens crack far more often than any other component fails). For a full breakdown of when the numbers favour AppleCare+ and when they don't, see Apple official vs third-party comparison.

Where you repair it matters too

Independent, non-franchise repairers are consistently the cheapest option for out-of-warranty repairs across every model we've priced, followed by authorised third-party chains, with Apple's own pricing at the top — that's the trade-off for guaranteed genuine parts and a warranty backed directly by Apple. FAQ

Common questions

Is it cheaper to fix an iPhone screen at Apple or a third-party shop?

Third-party and independent repairers are almost always cheaper — often $150–$300+ less on newer Pro models — but Apple and Apple Authorised Service Providers guarantee genuine parts with direct Apple backing. Either choice keeps your consumer guarantees intact: under the Australian Consumer Law, an independent repair does not void your warranty. your phone repair rights in Australia explain this in full.

Does replacing my iPhone battery void my warranty?

No. Under the Australian Consumer Law, an independent battery replacement does not void your manufacturer warranty or your consumer guarantees — only damage caused by the repair itself falls outside coverage. See your phone repair rights in Australia.

How do I know if it's my battery or something else draining fast?

Check Battery Health in Settings > Battery > Battery Health & Charging — if maximum capacity has dropped meaningfully below 80%, that's a strong sign the battery itself is the problem rather than an app or setting.
