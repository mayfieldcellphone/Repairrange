Samsung One UI 9: Where the Beta Stands in Australia

Samsung's One UI 9 rollout is picking up pace down under. The Galaxy S26 series kicked things off as the frontrunner of the One UI 9 beta program — now in its third beta cycle — and Samsung is expanding testing to a much wider range of Galaxy devices, including the S25 series and popular A-series models like the Galaxy A16 5G and A17 5G. For Australian owners, that means the next major Android update is closer than it looks, and it's heading to mid-range phones, not just flagships.

What is One UI 9?

One UI 9 is Samsung's custom layer on the latest version of Android (Android 17). It's more than a visual refresh: each release packs interface improvements, performance tuning, camera enhancements and security patches designed to keep your device fast, smooth and safe for longer. Samsung's test builds have already surfaced on its servers, and the firmware numbers — like A166BXXU8EZF1 for the Galaxy A16 5G and A256BXXUCFZF1 for the Galaxy A25 — are a reliable sign that device-specific testing is well underway.

One UI 9 beta in Australia: who's in, who's next

The Galaxy S26 series has been the beta pioneer, with Samsung now on its third One UI 9 beta build for the range. From there, the company is broadening the program. Based on what's been announced and what's appeared in testing, here's where the rollout stands:

  • In beta now: Galaxy S26 series.
  • Next in line (testing/development confirmed): Galaxy S25 series, S24 series, S23 series and Galaxy Z Fold7.
  • Mid-range A-series: Galaxy A56, A34, A25 (test build A256BXXUCFZF1), Galaxy A16 5G (test build A166BXXU8EZF1) and Galaxy A17 5G.

Both the A16 5G and the A25 recently received the stable One UI 8.5 update, so the jump to One UI 9 testing is the next step in their update cycle — good news if you're holding onto a mid-range phone rather than upgrading.

What to expect when One UI 9 lands on your Galaxy

Going by the beta builds and Samsung's update history, One UI 9 should bring a more intuitive interface, smoother navigation and improved performance under load, plus the security and camera refinements that come with a major Android release. For older devices, the optimisations usually mean better battery efficiency and responsiveness — which is exactly why keeping software current matters more than chasing new hardware.

When the update reaches your device, you'll get a notification, or you can check manually:

  1. Open Settings on your Galaxy.
  2. Scroll to Software Update.
  3. Tap Download and Install.

✅ Good to know: Run the update on Wi-Fi with decent battery charge to avoid interruptions mid-install, and back up your data before a major OS jump.

⚠️ Warning: Only update through your phone's built-in Software Update or Samsung's official channels. Third-party firmware sites can host malicious files that compromise your device's security.

Why updates + repairs are the real longevity play

A major OS update like One UI 9 makes your existing phone feel new — but only if the hardware underneath is healthy. A cracked screen, a battery that drains in half a day, or a port that won't charge will hold back any software upgrade. That's why the smartest upgrade path for most Australian owners is: update the software, repair the hardware. Conclusion

One UI 9 is shaping up to be one of Samsung's broadest updates in Australia — from the S26 series beta to A-series test builds, the message is clear: your current phone is getting the new software. Keep it updated, keep it repaired, and there's less reason to upgrade than you think.

FAQ

One UI 9 in Australia: common questions

When will One UI 9 come to Australia?

One UI 9 is already in beta in Australia for the Galaxy S26 series, and Samsung is testing builds for the S25 series and A-series models like the A16 5G and A25. No official Australian stable-release date has been confirmed, but the beta program and test builds point to a wider rollout soon.

Which Samsung devices get One UI 9 in Australia?

The Galaxy S26 series leads the beta. Samsung is also testing or developing One UI 9 for the S25, S24 and S23 series, the Galaxy Z Fold7, and the A-series — Galaxy A56, A34, A25, A16 5G and A17 5G.

Is One UI 9 based on Android 17?

Yes. The One UI 9 test builds on Samsung's servers are built on the latest Android platform (Android 17), bringing its interface, performance and security improvements into Samsung's custom layer.

Will One UI 9 slow down an older Galaxy like the A25 or A16 5G?

Usually the opposite. The A16 5G and A25 already run stable One UI 8.5 smoothly, and One UI 9 is being tested against these devices with optimisation in mind. A major update typically brings speed, battery-efficiency and security gains — which is exactly how older phones stay usable for years.

Do I need a new phone to get One UI 9?

No. One UI 9 is coming to existing devices, from the S26 series down to mid-range A-series phones. If yours is supported, you'll get it via Settings > Software Update. A healthy device handles major updates best, so a screen or battery repair before the jump can make the new OS feel like a new phone.
