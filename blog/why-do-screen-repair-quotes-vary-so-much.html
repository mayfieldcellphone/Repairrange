You might assume the shop charging more is simply greedy, or that the cheaper shop is cutting corners. The reality is usually more nuanced.

The Quality of the Spare Part

The single biggest factor in the cost of a screen repair is the quality of the replacement part. In the repair industry, there is a complex ecosystem of screen tiers:

  • Original Equipment Manufacturer (OEM): These are parts sourced directly from the manufacturer or through their authorised supply chains. They offer the exact colour calibration, brightness, and touch sensitivity you expect from a brand-new device. These are naturally the most expensive.
  • Refurbished Original: These are original screens that have had their cracked glass digitiser replaced. Because the actual LCD or OLED panel is the original, the display quality remains high, but the component has already seen service.
  • Third-Party High-Copy: These are screens manufactured by independent companies to replicate the specifications of the original. Modern high-copy screens are often excellent, but they may lack the specific hydrophobic coating or the exact colour depth of the original.
  • Budget Aftermarket: These are the lowest-cost options. They often use cheaper glass, lower-quality backlighting, or thinner ribbons. You will often notice a difference in touch response, screen brightness, or even the physical thickness of the frame, which can leave a gap between the screen and the chassis.

When you receive a quote, you are often paying for the quality tier the technician has chosen to stock. A shop that prioritises longevity and original-spec performance will always quote higher than a shop that uses the most affordable parts available to win a price war.

Labour Costs and Technician Expertise

Replacing a modern smartphone screen is no longer a simple matter of unscrewing a back panel. Modern devices are often glued together with industrial adhesives and feature complex, tightly packed internal components. Some repairs require the removal of the battery, the motherboard, and delicate facial recognition sensors.

A shop that charges more often invests in highly skilled labour. Experienced technicians have the steady hands and the knowledge base to navigate these delicate components without damaging the internal hardware. They also carry the appropriate insurance for their work. A shop that uses less experienced or junior staff may be able to quote lower prices because their overheads are lower, but you may be trading that for a higher risk of secondary damage, such as a loose cable or a compromised water seal.

The Cost of Specialised Equipment

Professional repair requires more than just a screwdriver. To perform a high-quality repair, shops need expensive tools: heat mats, precision suction jigs, micro-soldering stations, and vacuum lamination machines for refurbished screens. They also need specialised calibration software. If a repair shop is an authorised service provider, they are required to purchase specific diagnostic and calibration tools from the manufacturer to ensure the screen software matches the motherboard. This investment is significant and is naturally reflected in the service fee.

Warranty and Aftercare

When you pay for a repair, you are also paying for the safety net that follows it. A reputable shop stands behind its work. If the touch sensitivity starts acting up a week later or the screen develops a dead pixel, a quality shop will replace it without an argument. This warranty period is a cost to the business. Shops that quote rock-bottom prices often do so by removing the safety net—offering either no warranty or a very restrictive one. When you choose a quote, consider the value of knowing that the business will support you if something goes wrong.

The Hidden Costs of Overhead

Location dictates overhead. A shop located in a high-traffic retail centre or a prime high-street storefront has significantly higher rent and operating costs than a workshop located in a secondary location or an industrial area. These businesses must recoup their costs through their service fees. Sometimes, the difference between two quotes is simply the difference between the landlords' rent requirements.

How to Make an Informed Decision

Before you commit to a repair, do not just look at the figure at the bottom of the quote. Ask the right questions:

  • What grade of screen is being used? Ask if it is original, refurbished, or aftermarket.
  • What does the warranty cover? Does it cover ghost-touch issues or just dead pixels? How long is the warranty valid?
  • Will the water resistance be restored? A professional repair should involve replacing the adhesive gaskets that maintain the seal against moisture and dust.
  • Does the repair require calibration? For some modern devices, failure to use manufacturer software will result in the loss of features like True Tone or Face ID.

In the world of repairs, the phrase 'you get what you pay for' holds weight. If one quote is significantly lower than the others, it is usually because the business has made a deliberate choice to save money on parts, labour, or aftercare. Your goal as a consumer is to decide how much that risk is worth to you. If you rely on your phone for work or banking, paying for a high-quality, warrantied repair is usually the most economical choice in the long run. If the device is an older spare phone, a budget screen might be perfectly adequate. The choice should be yours, fully informed.
