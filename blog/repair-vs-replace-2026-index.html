3. The Environmental Impact: Carbon Footprint

Beyond the wallet, there is a significant planet-wide cost. 85% to 95% of a smartphone's carbon emissions occur during the manufacturing and shipping phases. By extending your phone's life by just one year, you can reduce its total annual carbon footprint by up to 30%.

Repairing is the ultimate "Green" choice. It prevents high-value materials (cobalt, lithium, gold) from entering landfills as e-waste and reduces the demand for destructive mining operations.

FAQ

Repair vs replace: common questions

Should I repair or replace my phone?

Repair if the phone is under about 3 years old, the repair is a screen, battery or port, and the quote is under 40% of the replacement cost. Replace if the phone is old, heavily damaged, no longer supported with updates, or the repair quote approaches its value. Our index page has the full cost maths.

When is a phone repair not worth it?

When the repair costs more than roughly half the price of a comparable new phone, when the phone is 4+ years old and out of software support, or when there's water damage plus a cracked screen (double-quote territory).

Does repairing an old phone extend its life?

Yes — a new battery typically restores a day of usage, and a screen replacement makes the device usable again. Repairing a 2–3 year old phone usually adds 1–2 years of life for a fraction of the replacement cost.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace a phone in 2026?

Repairing wins for most phones under 3 years old: $119–$379 for screen or battery against $1,000+ for a new flagship. Repair prices are at record highs in 2026 (see our 2026 market report), but replacement costs rose faster.

How do I decide if a phone is worth fixing?

Three numbers: the repair quote, the phone's current trade-in/resale value, and the price of the replacement you'd buy. If the quote is under 40% of replacement price and the phone still meets your needs, repair. If it's over 50%, replace. Our Apple vs third-party comparison helps with the quote side.
