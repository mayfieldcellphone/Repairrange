Goodbye to the Nothing Phone (1): Final Update Signals End of Software Support

RR
By RepairRange Editorial Team • July 04, 2026

Nothing Phone (1) Software Support Concludes

The Nothing Phone (1), which turned heads with its unique design and features, has officially reached the end of its software support lifecycle. First launched in July 2022 with Android 12, the device has now seen its final software update rollout confirmed by the company. This announcement signals a significant shift for users, especially those who embraced the brand’s fresh perspective in the ever-evolving smartphone market.

What This Final Update Means

While the Nothing Phone (1) has offered an impressive array of functionalities and a distinctive aesthetic, the end of its software support means that users will no longer receive updates or security patches. This could leave your device vulnerable to threats and may impact the overall user experience as apps continue to evolve and demand more from their operating systems.

Next Steps for Users

If you're a proud owner of the Nothing Phone (1), it’s essential to keep your device’s software current with the last update. Be sure to back up all your important data before proceeding with any updates. As you think about your future phone needs, consider exploring options that offer ongoing software support to safeguard your investment.

Looking Ahead

Though the Nothing Phone (1) was a bold entry into the smartphone landscape, it’s crucial for users to be aware of the lifecycle of their devices. For those reminiscing about their experience with Nothing, or contemplating a jump to another brand, stay vigilant about support longevity.

What the end of support means for Phone (1) owners

The end of support for the Phone (1) doesn't mean the phone is dead — it means no more guaranteed OS and security updates from Nothing. For owners, three things matter: (1) the phone keeps working exactly as it does today, (2) after the final update, new security patches stop, so avoid sideloading apps and dodgy links, and (3) when it eventually needs a screen or battery, parts are still available through independent repairers — the Phone (1)'s transparent design actually makes battery swaps relatively straightforward. If the phone still does what you need, a $89–$129 battery keeps it going; if you're upgrading, trade it in or sell it while it still turns on.
