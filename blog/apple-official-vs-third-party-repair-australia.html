Screen quality: why "cheaper" needs a qualifier

Independent shops quote different screen tiers — genuine OEM, good aftermarket, and budget. On iPhones, the cheapest aftermarket screens can lose Face ID or True Tone support because Apple pairs components to the logic board. That doesn't make independent repair bad — it makes tier choice the real decision. A $379 Apple screen and a $549 top-tier aftermarket screen are comparable quality; the $199 budget tier is the compromise. Our screen quality tiers guide walks through exactly what you lose at each tier.

Does a third-party repair void your warranty?

No — and this is the single most misunderstood part of phone repair in Australia. Under the Australian Consumer Law, Apple's manufacturer warranty and your consumer guarantees are separate. Having an independent shop repair your iPhone does not void them. The ACCC's position is clear: a supplier cannot refuse a warranty claim simply because a third party performed a repair. If a repairer tells you otherwise, they're wrong. Full detail: your phone repair rights in Australia.

⚠️ One honest caveat: if a third-party repair causes the fault (for example a botched screen install damages the logic board), that specific damage isn't covered. Your rights cover the original fault — not damage introduced by a bad repair. Choose your repairer accordingly.

When Apple authorised genuinely wins

  • Back glass: $159 vs $179–$349. Apple's laser removal equipment makes this the one repair where official service is both cheaper and cleaner.
  • AppleCare+ coverage: if your device is covered, the $29 screen fee beats everything — but it's only honoured at Apple authorised providers.
  • Guaranteed genuine parts with proper pairing — no tier lottery.
  • Newer models in the first 6 months: aftermarket parts supply is still maturing, so the indie price advantage is smallest right after launch.

When a good independent shop wins

  • Out-of-warranty screens and ports: 20–40% cheaper on the same quality tier.
  • Batteries on older models: $89–$149 vs Apple's $119+ — close, and on models Apple no longer services, independents are the only option.
  • Board-level work: Apple typically replaces whole modules or quotes past the device's value; independents do component-level repairs.
  • Speed: same-day is common at independents; Apple bookings often mean days.
  • Older iPhones (out of Apple support): official service isn't available at all — independent repair is the only path, and parts pricing is mature.

The decision, in one line

Back glass or AppleCare+ → Apple. Everything else out of warranty → a reputable independent shop, choosing a screen tier that matches what you'd want from Apple. And either way, your Australian Consumer Law rights stay intact.

FAQ

Apple vs third-party repair: common questions

Does a third-party repair void my Apple warranty in Australia?

No. Under the Australian Consumer Law, an independent repair does not void your warranty or consumer guarantees, and Apple cannot refuse a claim just because a third party repaired the device.

Is it cheaper to repair an iPhone at Apple or a third-party shop?

Screens and ports: independent shops are typically 20–40% cheaper. Batteries: near parity ($119 Apple vs $89–$149 indie). Back glass: Apple is cheaper ($159 vs $179–$349 indie).

Are third-party iPhone parts as good as Apple's?

Quality tiers exist. Good aftermarket and genuine OEM screens are comparable, but budget tiers can lose Face ID/True Tone pairing. Ask which tier you're quoted — the screen quality tiers guide explains it.

Does AppleCare+ cover third-party repairs?

No — AppleCare+ is honoured only at Apple stores and authorised providers. With a $29 flat screen fee on iPhone 17 models it's great value on Pro Max models, but you must use Apple's network.

When should I choose Apple over an independent shop?

Back glass, devices under AppleCare+, and any repair where you want guaranteed genuine parts. For everything else out of warranty, a reputable independent shop with a clear screen-tier quote is usually the smarter spend.
