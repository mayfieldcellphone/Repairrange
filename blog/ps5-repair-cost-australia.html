PS5 Repair Cost in Australia (2026): What You'll Actually Pay

PS5 repair pricing is quoted per fault rather than per screen or battery — and the most common fault isn't the disc drive, it's charging. Here's the full Australian breakdown.

PS5 console repair cost by fault

PS5 repair pricing is quoted very differently from phone repair — console repairers charge per fault rather than per "screen" or "battery," and the single most common complaint across Australian repairers isn't the disc drive or HDMI port, it's charging: both the console's own USB ports and, even more often, the DualSense controller's USB-C charging port wearing out or coming loose.

If your controller charges intermittently, needs the cable held at a specific angle, or has stopped charging entirely, this is almost always the USB-C port itself — a $10–20 part that costs $48–$69 fitted, which is one of the cheapest, highest-value console repairs available. It's very rarely a battery or motherboard fault when the symptom is "won't charge unless I wiggle the cable."

What actually moves the price

  • Console vs. controller: console repairs (HDMI, disc drive, power supply) are priced meaningfully higher than controller repairs, which mostly sit under $70.
  • Location: HDMI port repair specifically shows real city-to-city price spread in Australia — worth comparing local quotes rather than assuming a national flat rate.
  • Severity: a simple port swap is cheap; a fault that's reached the HDMI IC chip or motherboard pushes the same "HDMI repair" from ~$195 to $249+.
  • Mail-in vs. local: mail-in services can be cheaper on the headline price but add shipping cost and turnaround time — worth weighing against a same-day local repair, especially for a primary gaming device.

Is it worth repairing or replacing?

Because most PS5 faults are $99–$250 against a console that retails well above $700–$900, repair is almost always the cheaper option — this isn't a close call the way an old phone screen repair can be. The one exception is a console with multiple compounding faults (e.g. failed HDMI IC chip plus disc drive plus thermal issues) where repeated repair costs can approach a meaningful fraction of a replacement or upgraded model; get a full diagnostic rather than paying for faults one at a time if the console has more than one symptom. For controllers, repair is close to universally worth it — a $48–$69 charging port fix against a $100+ replacement DualSense is straightforward. FAQ

Common questions

My DualSense controller only charges if I hold the cable at an angle — what's wrong?

This is the classic symptom of a worn or loose USB-C charging port, not a battery fault. A $48–$69 port repair almost always resolves it.

Is a PS5 HDMI port repair worth it, or should I just buy a new console?

Nearly always worth it — even at the top of the city-pricing range (~$350), it's a fraction of a replacement console's cost, and the fault is isolated to one port rather than indicating a wider hardware problem in most cases.

Can I fix DualSense stick drift myself instead of paying for repair?

DIY cleaning fixes some early-stage drift, but persistent drift usually means the analog stick module itself needs replacing — professional repair (~$65–$99) is more reliable than repeated DIY attempts, especially since take-apart risks damaging the ribbon cables around the sticks.
