Phone Not Charging? Troubleshoot Before You Pay for Repairs

Published 22 June 2026 · Written by RepairRange Editorial · 6 min read

Your phone will not charge. Before you drive to a repair shop and spend $80+, there is a good chance you can fix this yourself in under five minutes. The majority of "dead charging port" diagnoses that come through repair shops turn out to be one of three simple problems: a dirty port, a bad cable, or a software glitch.

Work through these steps in order. Each one eliminates a possible cause, and if you reach the end without a fix, you will know the port genuinely needs replacing.

Step 1: Check the Cable and Charger

1

This is the most commonly overlooked cause. Try a different cable — ideally one you know works with another device. USB-C cables in particular degrade internally from repeated bending near the connector. The cable may look fine externally but have broken conductors inside.

Also try a different power adapter or USB port. Wall adapters fail more often than people expect, especially cheap third-party ones. Test with your laptop's USB port to eliminate the adapter as a variable.

If the phone charges with a different cable or adapter, the problem is solved — replace the cable, not the phone.

Step 2: Clean the Charging Port

2

This is the number one fix that repair technicians perform for "phone not charging" walk-ins. Pocket lint, dust, and fibres compact into the bottom of the charging port over months, preventing the cable from making a proper connection. The cable feels like it is plugging in, but it is actually sitting on top of a layer of compressed lint.

How to clean it:

Power off the phone. Use a wooden or plastic toothpick — never metal, as metal can short-circuit the port contacts or damage the pins. Gently scrape along the bottom of the port from one side to the other. You will be surprised how much compressed lint comes out.

Shine your phone's torch (use a second phone or a flashlight) into the port to check for remaining debris. You should be able to see clean metal contacts at the bottom. If you have compressed air, give it a few short bursts — but hold the can upright to avoid spraying propellant liquid.

Repair shops charge $20–$40 for port cleaning

If you are not comfortable cleaning the port yourself, any repair shop will do it for $20–$40. This is a legitimate service — they use specialised tools under magnification. But if you can manage a toothpick, you can save the trip. Mayfield Phone Repair in Newcastle offers free port cleaning for walk-in customers.

Step 3: Restart the Phone

3

Software glitches can prevent charging. A hard restart clears temporary bugs in the charging management system.

iPhone: Press and quickly release Volume Up, then Volume Down, then press and hold the Side button until the Apple logo appears.

Samsung / Android: Press and hold Power + Volume Down for 10–15 seconds until the phone restarts.

After restarting, plug in the charger again. If the phone now charges, the issue was software-related and is resolved.

Step 4: Check for "Moisture Detected" (Samsung)

4

Samsung phones have moisture sensors in the USB-C port that will block charging if they detect liquid or unusual conductivity. The phone displays a water droplet icon and refuses to charge.

If the port is genuinely dry and the warning persists, try drying it with compressed air and leaving the phone in a well-ventilated area for several hours. Persistent false moisture warnings after the port is completely dry usually indicate damaged sensors or corrosion inside the port — this requires professional inspection.

Step 5: Try Wireless Charging

5

If your phone supports wireless charging (most phones from 2020 onwards do), try placing it on a wireless charger. If it charges wirelessly but not via cable, the problem is definitively in the charging port or cable — not the battery, software, or charging circuit.

Wireless charging can also serve as a temporary workaround while you arrange a port repair or replacement.

Step 6: Check for Physical Damage

6

Shine a light into the charging port and look carefully at the pins and contacts. If pins are bent, broken, or blackened from corrosion, the port needs replacing. Also check whether the cable connector wobbles excessively when plugged in — a loose connection indicates the port's internal retention clips are worn out.

If the port is physically damaged, cleaning will not fix it. The port assembly (flex cable + connector) needs to be replaced.

Still Not Charging? The Port Needs Replacing

If you have tried all six steps and the phone still will not charge via cable, the charging port needs physical replacement. Here are your options.

Professional replacement: $60–$130

A technician replaces the USB-C or Lightning port flex cable in 30–60 minutes. Most independent shops in Australia charge $60–$100 for Samsung and $80–$130 for iPhones (iPhone ports are more complex to replace).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my phone not charging when plugged in?

The most common cause is lint compacted in the charging port. Try cleaning it with a wooden toothpick. Other causes include a faulty cable, damaged adapter, software glitch, or a worn port that needs replacement.

How do I clean my phone charging port?

Power off the phone. Use a wooden or plastic toothpick to gently scrape lint from the bottom of the port. Never use metal. Use short bursts of compressed air. Shine a torch in to check for remaining debris.

How much does charging port replacement cost in Australia?

Professional USB-C port replacement costs $60–$100 at most independent shops. DIY kits for Samsung Galaxy S-Series are $79 including tools. iPhone port replacement is more complex: $80–$130 professionally.

What does "moisture detected" mean on Samsung?

Samsung's sensors have detected liquid or unusual conductivity in the USB-C port. Dry it with compressed air, leave the phone in a ventilated area for several hours, and try again. Persistent warnings after thorough drying indicate damaged sensors or corrosion needing professional repair.
