Dr.Fone vs iMyFone: which phone recovery tool actually works?

By RepairRange · Independently researched pricing

You've probably seen ads for both. They promise to recover deleted photos, bypass locks, and fix system crashes. But which one delivers — and are either worth the money?

What Are Dr.Fone and iMyFone?

Both are desktop software suites (Windows and Mac) that connect to your iPhone or Android phone via USB. They market themselves as all-in-one phone management tools covering:

  • Data recovery — recover deleted photos, messages, contacts, WhatsApp chats
  • Screen unlock — bypass forgotten passcodes, remove iCloud/FRP locks
  • System repair — fix stuck-on-logo, boot loops, black screen issues
  • Phone transfer — move data between phones or backup to computer
  • WhatsApp transfer — move WhatsApp chats between iPhone and Android

Dr.Fone is made by Wondershare, a publicly-traded Chinese company (Shenzhen, est. 2003). It's the older and more established product — launched in 2011 and marketed heavily through YouTube sponsorships.

iMyFone is made by iMyFone Technology, a smaller Shenzhen-based competitor. They entered the market around 2015 and have grown through aggressive Google Ads and affiliate marketing.

The Honest Truth About Data Recovery Software

Here's what the ads don't tell you:

Neither tool can recover data that's been overwritten.

When you delete a photo, the phone marks that storage space as "available." If you keep using the phone — taking new photos, installing apps, receiving messages — that space gets overwritten. Once overwritten, no software on earth can recover the data. This is a fundamental law of flash storage, not a software limitation.

Both Dr.Fone and iMyFone work best when:

  • You stop using the phone immediately after data loss
  • The data was deleted recently (hours, not weeks)
  • You have an iTunes/iCloud backup (for iPhone) that the software can extract from
  • The phone still powers on and connects to a computer

What Real Repair Technicians Use Instead

In repair shops, these consumer tools are rarely used. Technicians typically use:

  • 3uTools (free) — Chinese-developed iOS flash/backup tool. The industry standard for iPhone firmware work. Not user-friendly, but powerful and free.
  • iMazing (~$70 AUD one-time) — Swiss-made iPhone backup browser. Extracts data from backups, exports messages as PDF, manages apps. More trustworthy privacy-wise than Chinese tools.
  • Itunes/Finder backups (free) — An encrypted iTunes backup captures almost everything. If you had backups enabled, you may not need any third-party tool.
  • Hardware readers — For truly deleted data, technicians use forensic tools like Cellebrite (law enforcement grade, $10,000+) or JCID/P10 (China-made, ~$200-$500). These read the NAND flash chip directly.

Which One Should You Choose?

Bottom line:

If you must buy one, Dr.Fone has better Android support (particularly for Samsung devices) and a longer track record. If you're only working with iPhones, iMyFone is slightly cheaper and the iOS recovery module is functionally identical.

But if your data loss is from physical damage — a cracked screen, water damage, or a phone that won't turn on — neither tool will help. You need a repair shop that can temporarily revive the phone to extract data. Software can't fix broken hardware.
