How to remove adware and pop-ups from your phone

By RepairRange · Independently researched pricing · Updated 15 August 2026

Pop-up ads on your home screen. Your browser redirecting to spam sites. Apps you never installed appearing out of nowhere. Here's how to get rid of it all — permanently.

First: Identify What You're Dealing With

Not all unwanted ads are "adware" (malware that injects ads). Most phone pop-ups fall into one of three categories:

Symptom Likely Cause Severity
Ads popping up on home screen, even when no app is openRogue app with overlay permissionHigh
Browser redirects to scam/spam sitesBrowser hijacker or malicious calendar subscriptionMedium
Ads inside a specific app onlyThat app's normal ad-supported modelLow — expected behaviour

The "test" to confirm it's adware:

If ads appear on your home screen or over other apps, it's almost certainly a rogue app. Legitimate apps can only show ads inside themselves.

How to Remove Adware from Android

Step 1: Boot into Safe Mode

Safe Mode temporarily disables all third-party apps. If the ads stop in Safe Mode, you've confirmed it's an app you installed.

  • Press and hold the power button until the power menu appears
  • Press and hold "Power off" until "Reboot to safe mode" appears
  • Tap OK. Your phone will restart with "Safe mode" in the corner

Step 2: Find the Rogue App

Go to Settings → Apps → See all apps. Look for:

  • Apps you don't remember installing
  • Apps with generic names like "System Update", "Wi-Fi Booster", "Battery Saver"
  • Apps with no icon (blank or generic Android icon)
  • Apps that use an unusually large amount of background data
  • Apps with "Draw over other apps" or "Display over other apps" permission enabled unnecessarily

Step 3: Remove Special Access

Before uninstalling, go to Settings → Apps → [rogue app] → Advanced → Display over other apps and toggle it OFF. This immediately stops the pop-ups.

Step 4: Uninstall

Tap Uninstall. If the button is greyed out (some aggressive adware blocks uninstall), go to Settings → Security → Device admin apps and deactivate the app there first, then uninstall.

Step 5: Clear Browser Data

If you had browser redirects, the adware may have modified your browser settings:

  • Chrome: Settings → Privacy → Clear browsing data → All time → Clear
  • Samsung Internet: Settings → Privacy → Delete browsing data → All time → Delete
  • Also check: Settings → Apps → Chrome → Storage → Clear data

How to Remove Adware from iPhone

True adware is rare on iPhone because iOS sandboxes apps and Apple reviews every App Store submission. What looks like "adware" on iPhone is usually:

Fix 1: Malicious Calendar Subscriptions

The #1 cause of iPhone "pop-ups." Spam websites trick you into subscribing to a calendar that then floods you with event notifications. Fix:

  • Settings → Calendar → Accounts → Subscribed Calendars
  • Look for any calendar you don't recognise — tap it → Delete Account

Fix 2: Rogue Configuration Profile

Some spam sites install a configuration profile that controls your phone's behaviour. Check:

  • Settings → General → VPN & Device Management
  • If you see a profile you don't recognise → tap it → Remove Profile

Fix 3: Safari Pop-Ups

If you get pop-ups only in Safari:

  • Settings → Safari → Clear History and Website Data
  • Then: Settings → Safari → Block Pop-ups (toggle ON)
  • Then: Settings → Safari → Advanced → Website Data → Remove All Website Data

Fix 4: Suspicious VPN Apps

Some free VPN apps inject ads into your browsing. Check Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → VPN. Delete any VPN you didn't intentionally install, then delete the app.

What Doesn't Work (Avoid These)

  • "Antivirus" apps for iPhone — They can't scan for viruses because iOS doesn't allow it. Any iOS "antivirus" app is just a placebo.
  • "Phone cleaner" apps on Android — Most of these ARE the adware. They show fake scan results to scare you and then show you ads.
  • Factory reset as first resort — Try the steps above first. A factory reset wipes everything, and you'll lose data you might have been able to save.

How to Prevent Adware in the Future

  • Only install apps from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store
  • Check app reviews before installing — sort by "Most recent" to catch recent adware complaints
  • Never click "Install" on pop-ups that appear while browsing — they're fake
  • On Android, keep Google Play Protect enabled (Play Store → Profile → Play Protect)
  • Don't install APK files from random websites — APKMirror is the only safe third-party source
  • If an app asks for "Display over other apps" permission and it's not a chat app or system tool, deny it
