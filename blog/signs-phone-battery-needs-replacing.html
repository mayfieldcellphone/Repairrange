7 Signs Your Phone Battery Needs Replacing

By RepairRange · Independently researched pricing

Published 22 June 2026 · Written by RepairRange Editorial · 7 min read

Phone batteries degrade invisibly. One day your phone lasts until bedtime, and six months later it is dying at 3pm. The decline is gradual enough that most people adapt their behaviour — carrying a charger everywhere, keeping a power bank in their bag — instead of addressing the root cause.

Here are the seven warning signs that your battery has crossed the threshold from "ageing" to "needs replacing." If you recognise three or more, it is time.

Sign 1: Battery Health Below 80%

1

This is the most objective measurement you have. Every modern phone tracks battery health as a percentage of original capacity.

iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging. Look at "Maximum Capacity." Apple considers anything below 80% to be a degraded battery and will suggest service.

Samsung: Settings → Battery and Device Care → Diagnostics → Phone Diagnostics → Battery Status. Samsung rates the battery as "Good," "Normal," or "Weak."

Google Pixel: Settings → Battery → Battery Health. Shows remaining capacity percentage.

Below 80% means your battery physically cannot hold as much charge as it used to. No software update, setting change, or optimisation will fix this — it is a hardware limitation.

Sign 2: Phone Dies Before the Day Ends

2

If your phone used to last a full day on a single charge and now struggles to make it past 3pm with the same usage patterns, the battery has degraded significantly. The key phrase here is "same usage patterns." If you started watching four hours of YouTube daily, that is not a battery problem — that is a usage problem.

Compare like-for-like. If your routine has not changed but your battery life has halved, the battery is the issue.

Sign 3: Sudden Percentage Drops

3

Your phone shows 40%, you open the camera, and suddenly it is at 12%. Or it jumps from 25% to "shutting down" with no warning. These erratic percentage readings indicate the battery can no longer accurately report its charge level because the cells have degraded unevenly.

This symptom is particularly common in phones that have been through extreme temperature exposure — left in a hot car in summer or used extensively in freezing conditions.

Sign 4: Phone Shuts Down with Battery Remaining

4

If your phone powers off at 15%, 20%, or even 30% battery, the battery can no longer deliver enough voltage under load to keep the processor running. The phone's protection circuit shuts everything down to prevent damage.

This is what caused Apple's "batterygate" throttling controversy — iPhones were shutting down unexpectedly because degraded batteries could not deliver peak current, so Apple started throttling processor speed to prevent it. The correct fix was always battery replacement, not software throttling.

Sign 5: Phone Gets Unusually Hot

5

Warmth during charging or intensive use is normal. Heat that makes the phone uncomfortable to hold during basic tasks — texting, scrolling social media, making calls — is not. A degraded battery has higher internal resistance, which means more energy is wasted as heat during both charging and discharging.

If the heat is localised to the back of the phone (where the battery sits) rather than the top (where the processor sits), the battery is the likely culprit.

Sign 6: Slow Charging or Stops at 80%

6

If your phone takes noticeably longer to charge than it used to, or consistently stops charging at 80% even with Optimised Charging disabled, the battery management system may be limiting charge to protect a degraded battery from overheating or swelling.

Note: iPhones with iOS 17+ have a deliberate 80% charging limit feature that can be enabled in settings. Check Settings → Battery → Charging Optimisation before assuming a hardware fault.

Sign 7: Physical Swelling

7

This is a safety issue

If your phone's screen is lifting away from the frame, or the back panel is bulging, the battery is swelling from internal gas buildup. Stop using the phone immediately. Do not charge it. Do not sleep near it. Take it to a professional repair shop. A swollen battery is a fire and explosion risk — this is not a DIY repair.

What to Do Next

If you recognised three or more of these signs, your battery needs replacing. You have two options.

Option 1: Professional replacement

A technician replaces the battery in 30–60 minutes. Cost: $60–$130 in Australia depending on model. The safest option, especially for phones with waterproofing seals that need to be properly reinstalled.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check my phone battery health?

On iPhone: Settings → Battery → Battery Health & Charging — look at Maximum Capacity. On Samsung: Settings → Battery and Device Care → Diagnostics. On Pixel: Settings → Battery → Battery Health. Below 80% means replacement is recommended.

How long should a phone battery last before needing replacement?

Most phone batteries retain 80% of original capacity after 500 complete charge cycles, typically 2–3 years of normal use. Heavy users who charge twice daily may need replacement after 18 months.

How much does battery replacement cost in Australia?

Professional replacement costs $60–$130 for iPhones and $70–$130 for Samsung Galaxy phones. DIY kits are available from $35–$79. Diagnosable batteries that restore Battery Health cost $99–$129.

Is a swollen phone battery dangerous?

Yes. A swollen battery is a fire and explosion risk. Stop using the phone, do not charge it, and take it to a repair shop immediately. Do not attempt to puncture or remove the battery yourself.
