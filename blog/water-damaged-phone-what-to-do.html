Water Damaged Phone — What to Do in the First 60 Minutes

By RepairRange · Independently researched pricing

Published 22 June 2026 · Written by RepairRange Editorial · 8 min read

Your phone just went for a swim. Maybe it slipped into the toilet, took a tumble into the pool, or got caught in a downpour. Whatever happened, the next 60 minutes are critical. What you do right now determines whether your phone survives or becomes a $1,500 paperweight.

This guide covers exactly what to do — and what not to do — when your phone gets wet. These steps apply to iPhones, Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel, OPPO, and every other smartphone.

The 3 Things That Kill Wet Phones

Most water damage deaths are caused by what the owner does after the phone gets wet — not the water itself. The three killers are: leaving the phone powered on, plugging it in to charge, and using a hair dryer. All three cause short circuits or heat damage that turn a recoverable situation into a permanent one.

Minute 0–5: The Emergency Response

1

Get the phone out of water immediately

Every second counts. The longer the phone is submerged, the more water penetrates the seals. Even IP68-rated phones are not waterproof — they are water resistant, and that resistance degrades over time, especially after drops or repairs.

2

Power off immediately

Hold the power button and shut it down. If the screen is unresponsive, force restart then power off. Water and electricity together cause corrosion and short circuits on the logic board. The phone being off prevents current from flowing through wet circuits.

3

Remove the case, SIM card, and any accessories

Take off the case — water gets trapped underneath. Pop out the SIM tray (use the pin tool or a paperclip). Remove any screen protectors if they are peeling. You want maximum airflow to every opening.

Minute 5–15: Dry the Exterior

4

Wipe the phone with a lint-free cloth

Gently dry every surface. Pay special attention to the charging port, headphone jack (if present), speaker grilles, and SIM tray slot. Tilt the phone in different directions to let water drain from openings. Do not shake vigorously — this can push water deeper inside.

5

Stand the phone upright with the charging port facing down

Gravity helps. Place the phone on a towel with the charging port pointing downward so water can drain naturally. Leave it in a well-ventilated area — not in direct sunlight or near heat sources.

Minute 15–60: The Critical Decision

Now you need to decide: can you get to a repair shop today, or do you need to wait?

Option A: Get to a repair shop (best outcome)

A professional technician will open the phone, displace the water, clean the logic board with isopropyl alcohol or an ultrasonic cleaner, and dry every component before reassembling. This gives you the highest chance of saving the phone with no lasting damage.

Option B: Can't get to a shop today

Leave the phone powered off in a dry, well-ventilated area for at least 48 hours. Do not charge it. Do not turn it on to "check if it works." Place it near a fan or in a room with airflow — but not on a heater, not in an oven, and not in direct sunlight. After 48 hours, take it to a professional before powering on.

What NOT to Do — The Myths

Do NOT put your phone in rice

This is the most persistent myth in phone repair. Rice does not effectively absorb moisture from inside a sealed phone. What it does do is introduce starch particles and dust into the charging port and speaker grilles, creating additional problems. Multiple studies and every professional repair technician will tell you the same thing: rice does not work.

Do NOT use a hair dryer or heat gun

Heat can warp adhesive seals, damage the battery, melt internal plastic components, and push moisture deeper into the logic board. The heat from a hair dryer — even on a "cool" setting — is enough to cause damage to temperature-sensitive components.

Do NOT charge the phone

Plugging in a wet phone is the single most damaging thing you can do. Electrical current flowing through wet circuits causes immediate corrosion and can fry the charging IC, Tristar chip, or logic board. If your phone shows a "moisture detected" warning, it is telling you not to charge for a reason.

Do NOT turn it on to "check if it works"

Resist the urge. Powering on a phone with residual moisture inside causes electrical shorts. The phone may boot successfully and then die permanently 30 seconds later because of a short circuit you just created. Wait for a professional to inspect it first.

What Does Professional Water Damage Repair Involve?

When you bring a water-damaged phone to a repair shop, the technician will typically follow this process.

First, they disassemble the phone completely — removing the screen, battery, logic board, cameras, and all flex cables. They inspect every component under magnification for signs of water ingress, corrosion, or mineral deposits.

The logic board goes into an ultrasonic cleaner — a bath of specialised cleaning solution that vibrates at high frequency to dislodge microscopic contaminants from the circuit board. This is the gold standard for water damage treatment and something you cannot replicate at home.

After cleaning, the board is dried with compressed air and inspected again. If there is visible corrosion on specific ICs or connectors, the technician will clean those areas with isopropyl alcohol and a brush under magnification. In severe cases, corroded components may need to be replaced using microsoldering.

Once everything is clean and dry, the phone is reassembled and tested. Success rates vary depending on how quickly the phone was powered off and how long it was exposed to water.

How Much Does Water Damage Repair Cost in Australia?

Water damage repair costs in Australia typically range from $80 to $250 depending on severity and device model. Here is a general breakdown.

Light water exposure (quick submersion, phone powered off immediately): $80–$120 for ultrasonic cleaning and inspection. High success rate.

Moderate water damage (submerged for several minutes, phone was on): $120–$180 for cleaning plus minor component replacement. Good success rate if treated within 24 hours.

Severe water damage (salt water, phone was charged while wet, left for days): $180–$250+ for cleaning plus microsoldering. Lower success rate — some boards may be unrecoverable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a water damaged phone be fixed?

Yes, in many cases. If you power off the phone immediately and avoid charging it, a professional technician can often save the device. Success rates drop significantly if the phone was left powered on or charged while wet. The sooner you get it to a professional, the better.

Should I put my wet phone in rice?

No. This is a myth that has been debunked by every professional repair technician and multiple scientific studies. Rice does not effectively absorb moisture from inside a phone and can introduce starch and dust into ports and speakers. Power off the phone, dry the exterior, and get it to a repair shop.

How long should I wait before turning on a wet phone?

At minimum 48 hours of air drying if you cannot get to a repair shop. However, the safest approach is to have a professional open, clean, and dry the internal components before powering on. Turning on a phone with residual moisture can cause a short circuit and permanent board damage.

Does the iPhone water resistance rating mean it is waterproof?

No. IP68 means the phone can resist water under controlled laboratory conditions — typically fresh water at a specific depth for a specific time. Real-world conditions (pool chemicals, salt water, soap, hot water, pressure from movement) are different. Apple's own warranty does not cover water damage, which tells you everything you need to know about how "waterproof" they consider it.

Need a charging port replacement after water damage?
