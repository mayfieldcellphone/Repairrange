New Features in Instagram Edits: Bilingual Captions and More

RR
By RepairRange Editorial Team • July 03, 2026

Instagram Edits Introduces Bilingual Captions

Instagram has made waves in the content creation world by launching its Edits app last January, a direct competitor to popular editing tools like CapCut. Now, Aussie users will be thrilled to learn that the app has updated its features, making it even easier to connect with audiences globally.

Automatic Translations for Your Captions

One of the standout additions to Instagram Edits is the support for bilingual captions. This handy feature automatically translates your captions into a secondary language, allowing you to broaden your reach and engage with a diverse audience.

Supported Languages for Captions

As of now, the app supports several popular languages, including:

  • English
  • Indonesian
  • Russian
  • Portuguese

This variety ensures that you can cater to a wide array of viewers, making your posts more accessible and inclusive.

Enhancing Your Content with New Templates

Alongside bilingual captions, Instagram Edits has also rolled out fresh templates for users to experiment with. These new designs can help make your videos stand out, provide a professional touch, and keep your content current and engaging.

Maximise Your Android and iPhone Editing Experience

Whether you’re using an Android device or an iPhone, Instagram Edits is the go-to app for creative editing solutions that cater to all skill levels. Take full advantage of these new features and transform your content into a stunning visual experience.

Why this matters if you create content

The practical takeaway for creators: tools that automate the boring parts of publishing — like bilingual captions — lower the barrier for reaching non-English audiences without extra editing time. And this is a reminder that these features arrive as app updates, not hardware upgrades: your current phone runs them fine, and a phone that's working doesn't need replacing for a software feature. When apps get heavier over time, the first thing to check isn't a new phone — it's the battery, which on a well-maintained device is the difference between a sluggish-feeling phone and a snappy one.
