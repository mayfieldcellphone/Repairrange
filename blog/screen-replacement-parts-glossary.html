Glossary · August 2026

What those screen-quality words actually mean

By RepairRange · Independently researched pricing

OEM pull. Unbranded. Seller refurbished. For parts. You see these terms on eBay, AliExpress, and repair part sites — but nobody explains them in plain English. Until now.

Written by a working technician

These definitions come from 10,000+ repairs. They reflect what you'll actually get in the box — not what a marketing department wants the word to mean.

OEM Pull

What it means: "OEM" stands for Original Equipment Manufacturer — the same company that made the original part for your phone. "Pull" means the screen was physically removed (pulled) from a genuine device.

An OEM pull screen is a genuine Apple, Samsung, or Google screen that came out of another phone — typically one that had a different fault (dead board, iCloud locked, etc.) but a working display.

Quality: Excellent

Genuine brightness, colour accuracy, and True Tone. Fingerprint/face sensors work as expected. This is the closest you'll get to a brand-new original screen without buying from the manufacturer.

Watch out for: The screen may have minor wear (micro-scratches from previous use). Always check the seller's grading — "Grade A" means near-mint, "Grade B" may have visible marks. Burn-in on OLED pulls is also possible if the donor phone had heavy use.

Unbranded

What it means: An aftermarket copy manufactured by a third party, with no brand markings on the flex cable or back of the panel. These are commonly made in Shenzhen factories and sold under generic listings.

"Unbranded" is essentially a generic aftermarket screen. It's not made by Apple/Samsung/Google, and it's not made by a known brand like Tianma or BOE. The quality varies wildly — some are decent, many are terrible.

Quality: Variable (usually low)

Colours are often washed out. Brightness is lower than original. Touch response may lag. True Tone and biometric sensors frequently don't work. These are the cheapest option for a reason.

When it's acceptable: On an old phone you're keeping as a backup. On a kids' device. When the phone is worth less than $100 and you just need it to work. Not recommended for daily-driver phones or anything with an OLED display.

Seller Refurbished

What it means: A used screen that the seller has cleaned, tested, and possibly repaired. "Refurbished" in the screen world usually means the original glass was removed and a new glass layer was laminated onto the original OLED/LCD panel.

This is a genuine panel with new glass — the underlying display (the part that produces the image) is original Samsung/LG/BOE, but the outermost glass layer is aftermarket. The lamination process matters enormously.

Quality: Good (if well done)

When properly refurbished with quality OCA (optically clear adhesive) and vacuum lamination, these are nearly indistinguishable from new. A poor refurb job shows bubbles, uneven backlight, or delamination within weeks.

Watch out for: The quality of refurbishment is invisible until you install it. Buy from sellers with clear return policies. If the price seems too good, the refurb process was probably rushed.

For Parts or Not Working

What it means: The screen is sold as-is from a broken or non-functional device. The seller makes no promises about whether the screen works, what condition it's in, or whether it can be repaired.

People buy "for parts" screens to harvest components — the backlight, the flex cables, the IC chips. You wouldn't install a "for parts" screen directly into a phone (well, you could, but you shouldn't).

Quality: Unknown — assume broken

These are for repair technicians who plan to refurbish the screen themselves. If you're buying a screen to install in your phone, this is NOT what you want.

Used

What it means: A screen that was pulled from a working device and sold without refurbishment. The seller has tested it and it works, but the glass is original — meaning it has whatever scratches or wear the previous owner put on it.

"Used" is basically OEM Pull but with no quality grading. You're getting a genuine screen, but the cosmetic condition could be anything from mint to heavily scratched.

Quality: Genuine but cosmetically variable

Always check photos of the actual item. "Used" without a grade is a gamble. If the seller won't show you the actual screen you're buying, walk away.

One More Term: INCELL vs OLED

INCELL: A budget LCD replacement screen. The touch layer is integrated into the LCD (hence "in-cell"). Cheaper to manufacture, lower power efficiency, and colours are less vibrant. Standard on phones that originally shipped with LCD screens (iPhone 11, iPhone SE, budget Androids).

OLED: Organic Light-Emitting Diode. Each pixel produces its own light — true blacks (pixels turn off), vivid colours, and better power efficiency. Most flagship phones since ~2019 use OLED. Aftermarket OLEDs vary in quality — some match the original, others are noticeably dimmer.
