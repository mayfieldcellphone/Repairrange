Should You Repair or Replace Your Phone?

Published 22 June 2026 · Written by RepairRange Editorial · 6 min read

Your screen is cracked, your battery dies by lunch, or your charging port only works if you hold the cable at a precise angle. The question every phone owner faces eventually: is it worth repairing, or should you just buy a new one?

The answer is almost always math. Here is how to work it out in under two minutes.

The 40% Rule

This is the simplest decision framework in phone repair, and it works for every situation.

If the repair costs less than 40% of buying a comparable replacement phone, repair it.

Why 40%? Because a repaired phone gives you at least another 1–2 years of life (often 3–4 with a battery swap). At 40% of a new phone's cost, you are paying less than one year's worth of depreciation on a new device. Anything below that threshold is financially obvious.

Example: iPhone 14 with a cracked screen

A refurbished iPhone 14 costs around $900 AUD. 40% of that is $360. An independent screen replacement costs $120–$180 (OLED quality). That is well under the threshold — repair it. You save $720+ and get the same phone back in an hour.

Example: iPhone XR with a cracked screen + dead battery

A refurbished iPhone XR costs around $300 AUD. 40% is $120. A screen replacement ($99) plus battery ($69) totals $168 — that is over the threshold. In this case, replacing the phone may make more sense, especially since you would get a newer model with better cameras, processor, and battery life.

When to Always Repair

Battery replacement — almost always worth it

Battery replacement is the single best value repair in phones. It costs $60–$130 at a shop or $35–$79 as a DIY kit, and it restores your phone to near-original performance. If your phone is less than 4 years old and otherwise works well, a new battery gives you another 2–3 years of life. The math never favours buying a $1,500 phone over an $80 battery swap.

Charging port replacement — usually worth it

USB-C port replacement runs $60–$100 at most shops. Unless your phone is ancient, this repair always falls well under the 40% threshold. Samsung Galaxy S-Series ports can be replaced as a DIY kit for $79.

Back glass replacement — worth it for newer models

Back glass typically costs $60–$150 to replace. On a phone worth $800+, this is a straightforward repair decision. On a phone worth $300, it becomes cosmetic — you can live with a cracked back if you use a case.

When to Consider Replacing

Multiple issues at once

A cracked screen alone is worth repairing. A cracked screen plus a dead battery plus a broken speaker starts stacking up. Add the individual repair costs and apply the 40% rule to the total.

Phone no longer receives software updates

A phone without security updates is vulnerable. In 2026, Apple supports iPhones for 6+ years, Samsung and Google for 7 years. If your phone has fallen off the update list, replacing it makes sense — not just for features, but for security.

Water damage with logic board corrosion

If a repair shop tells you the logic board has corrosion damage and the repair is $200+, the success rate drops and there is a risk of recurring issues. This is one scenario where replacement may be the safer bet, depending on the phone's value.

The Environmental Argument

Beyond money, there is a sustainability case. Manufacturing a new smartphone produces approximately 70kg of CO₂ emissions. Repairing a phone produces near zero. If the repair makes financial sense, it makes environmental sense too. Australia generates over 20 million kilograms of e-waste annually — repairing instead of replacing is one of the simplest things individuals can do to reduce that number.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is it worth repairing a phone vs buying a new one?

Use the 40% rule: if repair costs less than 40% of a comparable replacement phone, repair it. Most screen and battery repairs fall well under this threshold for any phone less than 4 years old.

Is it worth replacing a phone battery?

Almost always yes. Battery replacement costs $60–$130 while a new phone costs $400–$2,000. If the phone is under 4 years old and works well otherwise, a new battery adds 2–3 more years of life.

How many years should you keep a phone?

Most phones receive 5–7 years of software updates in 2026. With a battery replacement at year 2–3, a phone can easily last 5+ years. Replace based on need, not age.

Should I repair a 5 year old phone?

It depends on repair cost and whether it still gets updates. A $79 battery on a 5-year-old phone with security updates is good value. A $300 screen on the same phone probably is not.
