Data Methodology

How We Collect & Verify Repair Prices

RepairRange publishes independent Australian phone repair prices. This page explains exactly where the numbers come from, what "verified" means, and how often the data is updated — so you can judge the data yourself.

The short version

Every repair price on RepairRange is a range, not a single number. The low end of each range is a price we have verified from an actual independent repair shop. The top end is a researched estimate of what a premium shop in a capital city would charge. Both figures are 2026 AUD and GST inclusive.

We publish ranges because a single "the price" number would be a lie — the same screen repair genuinely costs different amounts at a suburban shop, a CBD shop, and a manufacturer. Our job is to narrow the range to the honest one, not to pretend the industry is uniform.

Where the numbers come from

  • Verified shop pricing (low end). We maintain a working relationship with independent repair technicians in Newcastle NSW and cross-check their current walk-in price lists. The low end of each range is a price you can actually be quoted today, not a figure we invented.
  • Manufacturer pricing (authorised service). Apple, Samsung, and Google publish official out-of-warranty repair prices. We compare these directly and quote them in the database where they exist.
  • Researched estimates (top end). For the upper bound, we combine parts-supplier pricing, published shop rate cards, and repairer input to estimate what a premium capital-city shop charges.
  • Community verification. When readers share recent quotes that fall outside our published ranges, we investigate and update. Prices that can't be verified are not published.

What the three screen tiers mean

  • Incell: budget LCD replacement with the touch layer fused in. Used on older iPhones and entry-level models. Lowest price, visibly different from OLED.
  • Aftermarket OLED: a new third-party OLED panel. Visually close to original — correct blacks and colour. The default choice for most repairs.
  • Refurbished OEM: the original panel, reclaimed from a salvage device and re-laminated with new glass. Equivalent to a genuine new part in every way that matters.

We list all three tiers for Apple models because all three are available in the Australian market. Most Android flagships only have a single part tier, which is why their price ranges are narrower.

Update cadence

  • Quarterly review of the full database against current shop pricing.
  • Event-driven updates when a new flagship launches (the database is typically updated within 2 weeks of a major release) or when a parts supply change shifts prices materially.
  • The database footer always shows the last verification date. Current: July 2026.

What we deliberately exclude

  • Extreme outlier quotes — the "$39 iPhone screen" that exists only as an upsell bait, and the "$1,200 screen" quoted to insurance companies. Neither represents what a normal customer pays.
  • Promotional pricing — a limited-time $89 iPhone screen sale is not a price, it's marketing. We note sales exist but don't bake them into ranges.
  • Non-Australian pricing converted to AUD at retail exchange rates, which misrepresents the local market.

Why we don't publish single "the price" figures

A price range is the honest representation of the Australian repair market. A shop's quote depends on its rent, its parts supplier, its warranty terms, and whether it transfers serial codes for True Tone and Face ID. Two shops on the same street routinely differ by 30–50%. Anyone who promises you one definitive number is selling you something — usually a booking.

How to correct an error

Repairers and consumers alike are welcome to challenge our data. If you have a current quote that falls outside a published range, email the details (model, repair type, shop, date, price) to info@repairrange.io. We verify every submission and publish corrections with a note.
