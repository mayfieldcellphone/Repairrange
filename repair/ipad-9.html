iPad (9th gen) screen replacement — glass and LCD are separate parts

The 9th gen is one of the last iPads with a non-laminated display. The touch glass and the LCD are two separate layers, and that changes the repair completely: if the glass is cracked but the display still works, only the touch glass needs replacing — $100–$200. If there are lines, black patches or no image at all, the LCD itself is replaced — $100–$200. That is a genuine saving versus laminated iPads, where a cracked glass means replacing the whole glass-and-LCD assembly. Touch ID lives in the home button and is paired to the device — it transfers across with the repair, so fingerprint unlock keeps working.

Battery replacement

iPad batteries are glued into the chassis and sit under the display, so a battery replacement is a careful bench job rather than a quick swap — $100–$200, usually same day. If your iPad drops charge fast, shuts down early, or the screen is lifting (a sign of a swollen battery), stop using it and book it in — a swollen battery is a safety issue. We fit a fresh cell and reseal the device.

Charging, water damage & software

This model charges over Lightning. If it won’t charge or accessories keep dropping out, it’s often the port or the cable — worth a free check before assuming the worst, and a port repair is a flat $100–$200. Liquid damage is quoted to the device (powered-off and in for a clean beats rice every time). Software and restore work — disabled iPads, boot loops, failed updates — is quoted to the job, usually turned around the same day.

Thinking of a DIY fix?

The non-laminated screen is friendlier for a glass-only DIY than newer iPads, but the rest of the device is still sealed, thin and heavily glued — cracked digitisers and punctured batteries are common first-timer outcomes. For anything beyond a careful glass swap, a proper bench setup with heat, suction and the right tools pays for itself.

FAQ

iPad (9th gen) repair questions

How much does an iPad (9th gen) screen replacement cost?

Because the 9th gen is non-laminated, glass and LCD are separate parts. Touch-glass replacement — cracked glass with the display still working — is $100–$200, and the LCD/display assembly is also $100–$200. Most screen repairs land in that range, with same-day turnaround.

The glass is cracked but the display still works — can you replace just the glass?

Yes. Non-laminated means the touch glass comes off separately from the LCD, so a glass-only repair is possible at $100–$200. On newer laminated iPads the whole display assembly must be replaced — this model is one of the last where a glass-only repair is an option.

Can the iPad (9th gen) battery be replaced?

Yes — $100–$200, usually same day. iPad batteries are glued in and sit under the screen, so it's a careful job, but replacing the cell is routine and restores full battery life.

Is it worth repairing an iPad (9th gen)?

Usually yes. A $100–$200 repair is a fraction of the cost of a new iPad, and this model is still plenty capable for streaming, browsing and light work. We'll give you an honest steer if it isn't worth it.
